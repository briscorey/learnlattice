## The Hook

Look around your classroom. Imagine filling it — floor to ceiling, wall to wall — with grains of rice.

**How many grains would that take?**

Do NOT calculate yet. Write down your gut estimate. Fold the paper. We will come back to it.

---

## The Method: Breaking It Down

You cannot count billions of grains. But you can:

1. **Count a small amount:** How many grains in 1 cubic centimetre? (Fill a 1cm cube with rice and count — approximately 15-20 grains)

2. **Measure the classroom:** Length × Width × Height in cm

3. **Calculate:** Grains per cm³ × Volume of room in cm³

### Example
- Classroom: 10m × 8m × 3m = 240 m³ = 240,000,000 cm³
- Grains per cm³: approximately 18
- Total: 18 × 240,000,000 = **4,320,000,000 grains**

That is over **4 billion** grains of rice.

---

## Putting It in Context

- World rice production per year: about 500 million tonnes
- Mass of 4.3 billion grains: approximately 100 tonnes
- Your classroom of rice = about **4 truckloads**
- It would feed approximately 200 people for a year

---

## Teaching Notes

This is a Fermi estimation — named after physicist Enrico Fermi, who was famous for making surprisingly accurate estimates from very little information. The skill is breaking an impossible question into measurable parts.

The gut estimate vs calculated answer comparison is always dramatic. Students who guess "a million" are off by a factor of 4,000. This viscerally demonstrates that human intuition fails with large numbers — and that mathematics is the correction.
