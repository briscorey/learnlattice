## The Hook

Your calculator is broken. Only four keys still work: **1**, **5**, **+**, **×**

Can you make every number from 1 to 30?

> **Challenge:** Find the **fewest key presses** for each number. For example, 6 = 1 + 5 uses 3 presses.

---

## Track Your Results

| Target | Expression | Key Presses |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 1 + 1 | 3 |
| 3 | 1 + 1 + 1 | 5 |
| 4 | ? | ? |
| 5 | 5 | 1 |
| 6 | 1 + 5 | 3 |
| 10 | 5 + 5 | 3 |
| 25 | 5 × 5 | 3 |

> **Which numbers are hardest to make efficiently?** Why?

---

## Extend the Thinking

- What is the **maximum** number of key presses needed for any number from 1-30?
- Are there numbers that are **impossible** to make? Why or why not?
- If you could add ONE more key (2, 3, or -), which would reduce your total key presses the most? Justify your choice with data.
- Can you find a pattern in which numbers need the most key presses?

---

## Teaching Notes

**BTC Practices Used:** Thinking task with accessible entry (everyone can make small numbers), random groups, vertical non-permanent surfaces. Competition element (fewest key presses) drives engagement.

**MYP Criterion B Connection:** Students investigate which numbers are "expensive" (many key presses), discover patterns, and can generalise about the role of multiplication vs addition in efficient number construction.

**MYP Criterion C Connection:** The table format requires systematic communication. The justification for which extra key is most useful requires clear mathematical argument.

**Low Floor:** Every student can make 1, 5, 6, 10 immediately. **High Ceiling:** Optimising key presses and proving impossibility/possibility leads to deep number theory reasoning.
