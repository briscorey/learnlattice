## Student Task Sheet

**Subject:** Mathematics | **Criterion:** D (Applying Mathematics in Real-Life Contexts) | **Time:** 55 minutes

**Instructions:** Your class is running a stall at the school fair. Read the scenario carefully, then answer all questions. Show your working and explain your reasoning. There may be more than one valid approach.

### The Scenario

Your class has a budget of **¥500** to set up a food stall at the school fair. You plan to sell two items: fruit cups and popcorn bags.

**Costs:**
- Fruit cups: ¥6 per cup to make (ingredients and packaging)
- Popcorn bags: ¥3 per bag to make (ingredients and packaging)
- Stall rental fee: ¥80 (fixed cost, paid regardless of sales)

**Last year's data** (from another class):
- They sold about 60 fruit cups at ¥10 each
- They sold about 90 popcorn bags at ¥5 each

Your class goal: make at least **¥300 profit** to donate to a local charity.

### Part A: Plan (Levels 1-4)

1. If your class makes 50 fruit cups and 80 popcorn bags, what is the total cost (including the stall rental fee)?

2. Using last year's prices (¥10 per fruit cup, ¥5 per popcorn bag), calculate the revenue if you sell **all** 50 fruit cups and **all** 80 popcorn bags.

3. Calculate the profit. Will you reach the ¥300 goal?

### Part B: Apply (Levels 3-6)

4. Your class wants to increase the profit by raising the price of fruit cups to ¥12 each.

   a) What is the new profit per fruit cup sold?

   b) What percentage profit does this represent on each fruit cup? Show your calculation.

   c) If raising the price means you only sell 40 fruit cups instead of 50, is the price increase worth it? Use calculations to justify your answer.

5. Your class has ¥500 to spend. The stall fee is ¥80, leaving ¥420 for ingredients.

   You want to make at least 40 fruit cups. How many popcorn bags can you also make with the remaining budget? Show your working.

### Part C: Evaluate (Levels 5-8)

6. Design your own pricing plan for the stall. Decide:
   - how many of each item to make (within the ¥500 budget)
   - what price to charge for each item
   - your expected profit

   Present your plan clearly with all calculations. Explain why you chose these numbers.

7. Your plan assumes you sell every item you make. In reality, a stall at last year's fair sold about 80% of its stock.

   a) Recalculate your expected profit if you only sell 80% of each item.

   b) Does your plan still meet the ¥300 charity goal? If not, what would you change? Explain the trade-off involved in your decision.


## Task-Specific Rubric

### Levels 1-2
The student identifies some mathematical information from the scenario. Attempts cost and revenue calculations but may make errors. Limited connection between the mathematics and the real-life context.

### Levels 3-4
The student correctly calculates costs, revenue, and profit for given quantities. Percentage profit is calculated with correct method. The student selects appropriate mathematical operations for the context.

### Levels 5-6
The student compares pricing strategies using calculations and makes a justified recommendation. Budget constraints are applied correctly. The student designs a coherent pricing plan with clear working and explains the reasoning behind their choices.

### Levels 7-8
The student evaluates their plan against realistic conditions (80% sales rate) and discusses trade-offs. The response shows awareness that mathematical models simplify reality. The student proposes adaptations and justifies them with reference to both the mathematics and the context. The distinction between mathematical optimality and practical feasibility is addressed.


## Marking Guide

### Part A

**Q1** (2 marks): Cost = (50 x 6) + (80 x 3) + 80 = 300 + 240 + 80 = ¥620. Note: this exceeds the ¥500 budget. Students may or may not notice this. If they flag it, award a bonus communication mark. (1 mark for ingredients cost, 1 mark for including stall fee)

Wait: 50 x 6 = 300, 80 x 3 = 240, 300 + 240 = 540 + 80 = 620. This exceeds the budget. This is intentional. Students who notice are demonstrating Criterion D awareness. If a student says "we cannot afford this plan," that is excellent mathematical reasoning in context.

**Q2** (2 marks): Revenue = (50 x 10) + (80 x 5) = 500 + 400 = ¥900. (1 mark per correct product)

**Q3** (2 marks): Profit = Revenue - Cost = 900 - 620 = ¥280. This does NOT reach the ¥300 goal. (1 mark for correct profit, 1 mark for comparing to the goal)

### Part B

**Q4a** (1 mark): New profit per fruit cup = 12 - 6 = ¥6 (previously 10 - 6 = ¥4).

**Q4b** (2 marks): Percentage profit = (6/6) x 100 = 100%. (1 mark for correct method: profit divided by cost price, 1 mark for correct answer)

**Q4c** (3 marks): At ¥12 selling 40: revenue from fruit cups = 40 x 12 = ¥480, cost = 40 x 6 = ¥240, profit from fruit cups = ¥240. At ¥10 selling 50: revenue = 50 x 10 = ¥500, cost = 50 x 6 = ¥300, profit = ¥200. The price increase gives MORE profit (¥240 vs ¥200) even with fewer sales. (1 mark for each calculation, 1 mark for clear comparison and conclusion)

**Q5** (2 marks): Budget for ingredients = 500 - 80 = ¥420. Fruit cups: 40 x 6 = ¥240. Remaining: 420 - 240 = ¥180. Popcorn bags: 180 / 3 = 60 bags. (1 mark for correct remaining budget, 1 mark for correct number of bags)

### Part C

**Q6** (4 marks): A complete plan includes: quantities of each item (1 mark), prices for each item (1 mark), total cost within ¥500 budget (1 mark), expected profit calculated correctly with reasoning for the choices (1 mark). Accept any valid plan. The quality of the justification matters more than the specific numbers.

**Q7a** (2 marks): Recalculate revenue using 80% of planned quantities. (1 mark for applying 80% correctly, 1 mark for correct adjusted profit)

**Q7b** (2 marks): Evaluate whether the goal is still met (1 mark). Discuss trade-offs: e.g. "I could lower the price to sell more, but that reduces margin" or "I could make fewer items to reduce waste, but that caps my revenue" (1 mark for a trade-off that shows mathematical reasoning, not just opinion).

**Total: 22 marks**
