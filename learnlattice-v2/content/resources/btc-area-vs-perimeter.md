## The Hook

You have exactly **12 square tiles**. Arrange them to make a flat shape (tiles must share full edges).

> **Challenge 1:** What is the **largest** perimeter you can make?
> **Challenge 2:** What is the **smallest** perimeter you can make?

---

## Investigate

Build at least 6 different shapes with 12 tiles and record:

| Shape Description | Area | Perimeter |
|---|---|---|
| 3 × 4 rectangle | 12 | 14 |
| 2 × 6 rectangle | 12 | 16 |
| 1 × 12 rectangle | 12 | 26 |
| L-shape | 12 | ? |
| T-shape | 12 | ? |
| Your design | 12 | ? |

> **What makes the perimeter larger?** What makes it smaller?

---

## The Pattern

**Maximum perimeter:** A long thin line (1 × 12) gives perimeter 26. Can you do better with a non-rectangular shape? (Try a zigzag pattern.)

**Minimum perimeter:** The most compact shape — closest to a square. For 12 tiles, a 3 × 4 rectangle gives 14. Can you do better?

> **Key insight:** Shared edges are hidden from the perimeter. Every time two tiles share an edge, the total perimeter decreases by 2. Maximum perimeter = minimum shared edges. Minimum perimeter = maximum shared edges.

---

## Extend to Circles

Why are bubbles round? Because a circle has the **minimum perimeter for a given area** (or equivalently, the maximum area for a given perimeter). This is the **isoperimetric inequality** — one of the deepest results in geometry.

---

## Teaching Notes

**BTC Practices Used:** Manipulative-based entry (physical tiles or grid paper), random groups, vertical non-permanent surfaces. Students build before they calculate.

**MYP Criterion B Connection:** Students investigate the relationship between shape and perimeter for fixed area, discover the pattern (compactness minimises perimeter), and justify using shared-edge reasoning.

**MYP Criterion C Connection:** The systematic table recording and clear description of shapes require strong mathematical communication.

**Common Misconception:** Many students (and adults) believe area and perimeter always increase together. This task directly confronts and corrects that misconception.
