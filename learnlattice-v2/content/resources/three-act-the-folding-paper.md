## Act 1: The Hook

Take a piece of paper. Fold it in half. And again. And again.

> **How many times can you fold it?** Try. Most people cannot fold it more than 7 times.

Now imagine you COULD keep folding. **How thick would the paper be after 42 folds?**

Write your estimate. Most people guess a few metres.

---

## Act 2: Calculate

A sheet of paper is approximately 0.1 mm (0.0001 m) thick.

| Folds | Layers | Thickness |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 0.1 mm |
| 1 | 2 | 0.2 mm |
| 2 | 4 | 0.4 mm |
| 5 | 32 | 3.2 mm |
| 10 | 1,024 | ~10 cm |
| 20 | 1,048,576 | ~105 m (taller than Big Ben) |
| 30 | ~1 billion | ~107 km (¼ of the way to the Moon) |
| 42 | ? | ? |

> **Formula:** Thickness after n folds = 0.0001 × 2ⁿ metres

42 folds = 0.0001 × 2⁴² = 0.0001 × 4,398,046,511,104 ≈ **439,805 km**

---

## Act 3: The Reveal

After 42 folds, the paper would be approximately **440,000 km thick**. further than the distance from the Earth to the Moon (384,400 km).

> **A single sheet of paper, folded 42 times, reaches the Moon.**

---

## The Sequel

- After how many folds does the paper reach the height of your school? Your city's tallest building? A commercial aeroplane?
- Why CAN'T you fold paper more than about 7 times? (The paper's length must be at least π × thickness for each fold.)
- In 2002, Britney Gallivan folded a single sheet of paper 12 times. She derived the formula: L = (πt/6)(2ⁿ + 4)(2ⁿ − 1), where L is the minimum length of paper needed. How long was her paper?
- **Connection to data storage:** If you could halve the size of a computer chip 42 times, starting from 1 cm, how small would it be? (This is essentially Moore's Law in reverse.)

---

## Teaching Notes

**3-Act Structure:** Act 1 is physical (everyone folds paper). Act 2 is computational (exponential growth). Act 3 is revelatory (the Moon comparison).

**MYP Criterion D Connection:** The physical impossibility of folding paper more than 7 times vs. the mathematical possibility of reaching the Moon creates a rich discussion about the difference between mathematical models and physical reality.

**MYP Criterion B Connection:** Students investigate the pattern of doubling layers, describe it as a power of 2, and verify their formula.

**Global Context. Orientation in Space and Time:** The comparison to astronomical distances gives students a visceral sense of scale and the power of exponential growth.

**EAL Scaffold:** The physical folding activity requires no English. The table structure makes the mathematical pattern visible. Provide a reference card with km equivalents (height of school, aeroplane, Moon).
