## The Task

Students investigated the effect of temperature on the rate of dissolving sugar in water. They must present their findings as a lab report.

---

## Student Response A. Achievement Level 3-4

### What the student wrote:

> **Method:** We put sugar in water at different temperatures and timed it.
>
> **Results:** Hot water = fast. Cold water = slow. Medium water = medium speed.
>
> **Conclusion:** Hot water dissolves sugar faster.

### Annotation: Why This Is Level 3-4

The report has a structure (method, results, conclusion) but every section is incomplete. The method lacks quantities, equipment, and controlled variables. The results are qualitative. "fast" and "slow" instead of measured times. The conclusion is correct but unsupported by specific data.

**Teaching move:** Ask "If I wanted to repeat your experiment, could I? What information would I need?"

---

## Student Response B. Achievement Level 5-6

### What the student wrote:

> **Aim:** To investigate how temperature affects the time taken for 10g of sugar to dissolve in 200mL of water.
>
> **Method:** We heated water to 5 different temperatures (20°C, 40°C, 60°C, 80°C, 100°C). For each temperature, we added 10g of sugar and timed how long it took to dissolve completely. We stirred at the same speed each time.
>
> **Results:**
>
> | Temperature (°C) | Time to dissolve (s) |
> |---|---|
> | 20 | 145 |
> | 40 | 78 |
> | 60 | 42 |
> | 80 | 19 |
> | 100 | 8 |
>
> [Graph: line graph showing temperature vs. time]
>
> **Conclusion:** As temperature increases, the time to dissolve decreases. This is because higher temperature gives particles more kinetic energy, so they collide more frequently and with more force, breaking apart the sugar crystals faster.

### Annotation: Why This Is Level 5-6

The report is well-structured with correct terminology. The method includes quantities and controlled variables. Data is presented in a table AND a graph. The conclusion connects to particle theory. Missing: no discussion of reliability (only 1 trial per temperature), no evaluation of the graph type choice, no units on axis labels mentioned.

---

## Student Response C. Achievement Level 7-8

### What the student wrote:

> **Aim:** To determine the relationship between water temperature and the dissolution rate of sucrose.
>
> **Method:** [detailed method with 3 trials per temperature, controlled variables listed, equipment specified]
>
> **Results:** [table with mean times and range for each temperature]
>
> I chose a scatter plot rather than a line graph because the data points represent discrete measurements, not a continuous function. The curve of best fit suggests an exponential decay relationship rather than a linear one.
>
> **Evaluation:** The range of results at 20°C (145s, 152s, 138s) was larger than at 100°C (8s, 9s, 8s), which suggests that at lower temperatures, factors like inconsistent stirring have a greater effect on the outcome. Future improvements: use a magnetic stirrer for consistent agitation and increase to 5 trials per temperature.

### Annotation: Why This Is Level 7-8

The student justifies their choice of graph type ("scatter plot rather than line graph because..."). They evaluate the reliability of their own data by comparing ranges across temperatures. They identify a specific source of error and propose a concrete improvement. The communication is precise enough that another scientist could repeat the experiment exactly.

**The sentence that makes it Level 7:** *"I chose a scatter plot rather than a line graph because the data points represent discrete measurements, not a continuous function."* This shows the student choosing the representation, not just using the one they were told to use.
