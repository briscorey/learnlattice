## The Hook

Two satellite images. Same location. Twenty years apart.

In the first image: a winding river, dense forest, green everywhere.

In the second: a dam, a reservoir, roads, cleared land, a town.

The valley was home to 200+ species. Now it is home to 50,000 people.

**Was it worth it?**

---

## The Investigation

### Step 1: Observe the Change

Compare before-and-after images of a major development. Use Google Earth Timelapse or printed satellite photos.

Record every visible change:
- Land cover (forest → cleared, wetland → drained)
- Water features (river diverted, lake created)
- Human structures (roads, buildings, dams)
- Vegetation patterns

### Step 3: Evaluate

This is the hard part. The development created real human benefits AND real ecological costs. Your job is not to say it was good or bad — your job is to **weigh the evidence and make a reasoned judgement.**

---

## The Criterion D Connection

This investigation is built for Criterion D — reflecting on the implications of science.

Strong Criterion D responses:
- Use **specific data** (not just "biodiversity decreased" but "bird species decreased from 45 to 28, a 38% decline")
- Acknowledge **both sides** of the argument
- Propose **realistic mitigation strategies** based on evidence
- Consider **who benefits and who bears the cost**

## Teaching Notes

The Three Gorges Dam is the most powerful case study for schools in China. The data is publicly available: 1,300+ villages submerged, 1.3 million people relocated, 6,000+ plant species affected — but also 22,500 MW of clean power, massive flood protection, and improved navigation. The trade-offs are genuinely complex.

Alternatives if you want something smaller-scale: the Yangtze River dolphin extinction, Nanjing urban sprawl over the past 30 years (use Google Earth), or any local construction project that replaced green space.

The structured debate format works well: divide the class in half, one side argues FOR the development, the other AGAINST. Then swap sides. Students who have to argue the opposite position develop deeper understanding.
