## Overview

This worksheet extends the [Grade 6 Fractions, Decimals, and Percent Unit](/resources/grade-6-fractions-decimals-and-percent-unit/) with structured practice moving from equivalent fractions through operations and into cross-form conversions. Every section uses a real-world context so students are applying — not just calculating.

---

## Section A — Equivalent Fractions

**A1.** Complete each equivalent fraction.

a) 2/3 = __/12 b) 5/8 = 15/__ c) 4/5 = __/20 d) 7/__ = 21/27

**A2.** Simplify each fraction to lowest terms. Show what you divided by.

a) 8/12 = __ (divided by __) b) 15/25 = __ c) 18/24 = __ d) 36/48 = __

**A3. Spot the error.**
A student wrote: "⅖ = 4/8 because 2 × 2 = 4 and 5 + 2 = 7, wait that's wrong, let me try 5 × 2 = 10, so ⅖ = 4/10."
Is 4/10 correct? Explain *why* the method works or doesn't work.

---

## Section B — Comparing and Ordering

**B1.** Use < , > , or = to compare each pair. Show your working.

a) ⅗ __ ⅔ b) ¾ __ 7/10 c) 5/6 __ 9/11 d) 4/9 __ 4/7

**B2.** Order these fractions from smallest to largest. Show a common denominator.

7/12, ½, ⅔, 5/8, ¾

**B3. Real context.**
Four friends ran different distances on a trail:
- Amara: ¾ km
- Ben: 5/8 km
- Cora: 7/10 km
- Dan: 2/3 km

Rank them from shortest to longest run. Show your method clearly.

---

## Section C — Addition and Subtraction

**C1.** Calculate. Show every step including finding the common denominator.

a) ½ + ⅓ b) ¾ − ⅝ c) 5/6 + 7/9 d) 11/12 − 3/8

**C2.** Mixed numbers. Convert to improper fractions first, then calculate.

a) 2⅓ + 1¾ b) 3½ − 1⅔ c) 4¼ + 2⅝

**C3. Real context — cooking.**
A muffin recipe needs ¾ cup flour for the batter and ⅔ cup for the streusel topping.

a) How much flour in total?
b) You have 2¼ cups of flour. After making the muffins, how much is left?
c) You want to make 2 batches. How much flour do you need in total?

---

| | | 60% |
| ⅓ | | |

**D2.** Convert each. Show your method.

a) 7/8 as a decimal and percent.
b) 0.64 as a fraction in lowest terms.
c) 35% as a fraction and decimal.
d) ⅔ as a decimal (round to 3 decimal places) and explain why it's not exact.

**D3. Real context — survey data.**
A class of 28 students was asked their favourite subject.
- Mathematics: 7 students
- Science: 10 students
- English: 6 students
- Other: 5 students

Express each result as a fraction, decimal (2 d.p.), and percentage (1 d.p.).
Which form would you use in a school newsletter? Justify your choice.

---

## Section E — Problem Solving

**E1.** A pizza is cut into 12 equal slices. Omar eats ¼ of the pizza, Sofia eats ⅓, and Kai eats 2/12.
a) How many slices does each person eat?
b) What fraction of the pizza is left?
c) If the remaining pizza is shared equally among 3 people, what fraction of the whole pizza does each person get?

**E2.** A school charity raised $1,260. They donated ⅖ to a local shelter, ¼ to an environmental fund, and kept the rest for next year's event.
a) How much went to each cause?
b) What fraction was kept? What percent is that?

**E3. Reflection question.**
"Fractions, decimals, and percentages all represent the same thing — so we don't need all three forms."
Do you agree or disagree? Give two specific examples from real life where one form is clearly more useful than the others.
