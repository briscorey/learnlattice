## Overview

Students play a simple two-dice game, collect experimental data, then compare it to the theoretical sample space to determine fairness.

## The Investigation

### The Game
Two players take turns rolling two dice and adding the results.
- Player A scores a point if the sum is **even**
- Player B scores a point if the sum is **odd**

Play 36 rounds and record results.

### Phase 1 — Experimental Data
- Tally results: how many times did Player A win vs Player B?
- Calculate experimental probability for each player
- "Does this feel fair? What does the data say?"

### Phase 2 — Theoretical Analysis
- Draw the complete 6×6 sample space (36 outcomes)
- Count: how many outcomes give an even sum? How many give an odd sum?
- Even sums: 18/36 = 50%. Odd sums: 18/36 = 50%.
- "The game IS fair. Does your experimental data match?"

### Phase 3 — Design an Unfair Game
- Modify the rules to create a game that LOOKS fair but ISN'T
- Example: "Player A wins if the product is even, Player B wins if odd"
- Students investigate the new sample space and prove the bias

## Criterion B Alignment

| Strand | Evidence |
|--------|----------|
| Select a method | Design data collection (number of trials, recording method) |
| Apply strategies | Use sample space diagrams and experimental data |
| Describe patterns | Compare experimental vs theoretical probabilities |
| Verify | Test whether modified games match predicted fairness |

## EAL Scaffolds
- **Sentence frame**: "The theoretical probability is ___, but our experiment gave ___. This is because..."
- **Word bank**: fair, biased, sample space, experimental, theoretical, outcome, equally likely

## Differentiation
- **Approaching**: Use the provided sample space grid, focus on counting
- **Meeting**: Create own sample space, compare experimental to theoretical
- **Exceeding**: Design and prove an unfair game, explain the discrepancy mathematically
