## The Hook

Clear a space in the classroom. Put a piece of tape on the floor. That is **zero**.

Numbers to the right are positive. Numbers to the left are negative.

**Walk to your answer:**
- Start at zero. Walk 4 steps right. Where are you? (+4)
- Now walk 6 steps left. Where are you? (-2)
- You just calculated: 4 - 6 = -2. **With your feet.**

---

## The Rules of Walking

| Operation | Walking rule |
|---|---|
| Add a positive number | Walk RIGHT |
| Add a negative number | Walk LEFT |
| Subtract a positive | Walk LEFT |
| Subtract a negative | Turn around and walk RIGHT |

### The tricky one: subtracting a negative

Start at +2. Subtract negative 3.

"Subtract" means turn around. "Negative" means face left. Turn around from facing left = face right. Walk 3 steps right.

+2 - (-3) = +5. **Subtracting a negative is the same as adding.**

> This is confusing with symbols but obvious when you walk it. Your body understands what your brain resists.

## Challenges

Walk each calculation. End at the correct position.

1. 5 + (-7) = ?
2. -3 + 8 = ?
3. -4 - 3 = ?
4. -2 - (-5) = ?
5. 0 - (-1) = ?

## Teaching Notes

This is Bruner's enactive mode — learning through physical movement. Students who struggle with -3 - (-5) on paper will walk to the correct answer and then understand the symbols.

Tape a number line on the classroom floor (or use the corridor). Use it all year — every time a negative number question arises, send a student to walk it. The physical memory persists long after the lesson ends.
