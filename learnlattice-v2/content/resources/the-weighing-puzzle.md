## The Hook

Place objects on a balance scale. Find combinations that balance.

**Now the challenge:** Using ONLY the balance relationships you found, determine the weight of one object you never directly weighed.

This is algebra. The scale is the equals sign. The objects are the variables. And you are solving equations with your hands.

---

## The Puzzles

### Puzzle 1 (One equation)
A bag of marbles (m) balances 4 erasers (e).
If one eraser weighs 20g, how much does the bag weigh?

$$m = 4e = 4 \times 20 = 80g$$

### Puzzle 2 (Two equations)
- 2 bags + 1 pencil case = 5 erasers
- 1 bag = 2 erasers

Substitute: 2(2 erasers) + 1 pencil case = 5 erasers → pencil case = 1 eraser

### Puzzle 3 (The real challenge)
- 3 apples = 2 bananas + 1 cherry
- 1 apple + 1 cherry = 2 bananas

Find the weight of each fruit if the cherry weighs 30g.

---

## From Scales to Symbols

Every balance gives you an equation. Every time you "remove the same thing from both sides," you are keeping the scale balanced.

| Scale action | Algebra action |
|---|---|
| Remove 2 erasers from both sides | Subtract 2e from both sides |
| Double everything on both sides | Multiply both sides by 2 |
| Replace a bag with its equivalent | Substitute m = 4e |

---

## Teaching Notes

This is the definitive Bruner investigation for algebra. The balance scale is the concrete model that makes equation-solving intuitive. Students who struggle with "subtract 3 from both sides" have no trouble understanding "remove 3 erasers from both pans."

Use a real balance scale if available (even a ruler on a fulcrum works). The physical manipulation creates muscle memory that transfers to symbolic manipulation later.
