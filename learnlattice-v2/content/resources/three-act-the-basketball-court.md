## Act 1: The Hook

*Describe or sketch this scenario:*

A basketball player catches the ball on her own baseline. She sprints the full length of the court for a fast break layup, weaving around one defender at half court.

> **The Question:** How far did she actually run?

Estimate. Write it down. Is it more or less than the length of the court?

---

## Act 2: What Do You Need to Know?

Reveal on request:
- The court is 28 metres long and 15 metres wide.
- She starts at position (0, 3). on her baseline, 3 metres from the sideline.
- She runs diagonally to half court at position (14, 12) to receive a pass.
- She curves around a defender, passing through approximately (18, 8).
- She finishes at the opposite basket at position (28, 7.5).

> **Calculate the total distance.** Use Pythagoras' theorem for each segment.

**Segment 1:** (0,3) to (14,12): √(14² + 9²) = √(196 + 81) = √277 ≈ 16.6 m

**Segment 2:** (14,12) to (18,8): √(4² + 4²) = √32 ≈ 5.7 m

**Segment 3:** (18,8) to (28,7.5): √(10² + 0.5²) = √100.25 ≈ 10.0 m

**Total ≈ 32.3 metres**. more than the court length of 28 m.

---

## Act 3: The Reveal

The player ran approximately **32 metres**. about 15% further than the court length.

> **Discussion:** Why is the actual distance always MORE than the straight-line court length? Can you ever run exactly 28 metres from baseline to baseline?

---

## The Sequel

- GPS trackers show elite basketball players run 4-6 km per game. If a game has 40 minutes of actual play and the average speed is 5 m/s during movement, how much of the game is the player actually running vs. standing?
- How would the distance change if she took a different route around the defender?
- What is the SHORTEST possible path from baseline to basket while going around a defender at (14, 7.5)?

---

## Teaching Notes

**3-Act Structure:** Act 1 creates the question. Act 2 requires coordinate geometry and Pythagoras' theorem applied in context. Act 3 validates and opens discussion.

**MYP Criterion D Connection:** The task requires applying Pythagoras in a genuine athletic context and evaluating the model's limitations (real paths are curved, not straight segments).

**MYP Criterion C Connection:** Students must communicate their working clearly, label coordinates, and show each calculation step.

**EAL Scaffold:** Draw the court on the whiteboard with coordinates marked. The visual representation makes the problem accessible regardless of language level.
