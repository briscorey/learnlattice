## How to Use This in a Lesson
### As a Hook (5 minutes)
Project the game. Show a simple equation (like H₂ + O₂ → H₂O) with coefficient 1 on everything. Ask: **"What's wrong with this equation?"** Students who know conservation of mass will spot it. Students who don't will learn it in the next 30 seconds.
### As Practice (15 minutes)
Students work individually or in pairs. The game progresses from easy (single element reactions) through medium (combustion of methane) to hard (thermite reaction, ethane combustion). The real-time atom counter means students get instant feedback without waiting for the teacher.
### Connection to The Disappearing Candle
This game pairs directly with [The Disappearing Candle](/resources/the-disappearing-candle-chemistry-investigation/) investigation. Students who have balanced equations here will understand *why* the candle's mass appears to change — because the gaseous products escape.

## Controls
**Left click** a coefficient to increase it (cycles 1→9). **Right click** to decrease. The atom counter updates instantly — green means balanced, amber means keep working.
