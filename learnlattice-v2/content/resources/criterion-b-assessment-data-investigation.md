## The Assessment Task

### Dataset
Students receive a spreadsheet with data from 60 students containing: age (12-16), gender, daily screen time (hours), hours of sleep per night, number of siblings, distance to school (km), and favourite subject.

*(Teachers: use your own class survey data, or generate a realistic dataset. The specific values don't matter — the investigation design does.)*

### Questions

**(a)** Formulate a statistical question that can be investigated using this dataset. Your question should involve comparing groups or exploring a relationship between variables. *[Criterion B strand i]*

**(b)** Select and create appropriate data displays (graphs/charts) to investigate your question. Calculate relevant statistics (measures of central tendency and/or spread). *[Criterion B strand i, ii]*

**(c)** Describe the patterns you observe in your data displays and calculations. *[Criterion B strand ii]*

**(d)** Answer your original question using evidence from your analysis. Discuss the reliability of your conclusion — what are the limitations? What would you do differently with more time or data? *[Criterion B strand iii]*

## Annotated Student Response: Levels 3–4

> **Student A's question:** "What is the average screen time?"
>
> **Display:** A single bar chart showing average screen time = 4.2 hours
>
> **Analysis:** "The average screen time is 4.2 hours. This is quite a lot."
>
> **Conclusion:** "Students spend a lot of time on screens."

### Why this is Level 3–4:

The question is not investigative — it can be answered with a single calculation. There is no comparison, no relationship explored, no subgroups analysed. The display is appropriate (bar chart) but minimal. The conclusion restates the finding without any deeper analysis or acknowledgment of limitations.

**What's missing:** A comparative or relational question, multiple displays, measures of spread, pattern description, and critical evaluation.

---

## Annotated Student Response: Levels 5–6

> **Student B's question:** "Do students who get more sleep spend less time on screens?"
>
> **Display:** A scatter plot of sleep (x-axis) vs screen time (y-axis) with a line of best fit. A table showing mean screen time for students sleeping <7 hours, 7-8 hours, and >8 hours.
>
> **Analysis:** "The scatter plot shows a weak negative trend — students who sleep more tend to have slightly lower screen time, but there is a lot of variation. The mean screen time for the <7 hours sleep group is 5.1 hours, while the >8 hours sleep group averages 3.6 hours."
>
> **Conclusion:** "There is a relationship between sleep and screen time, but it is not strong. Students who sleep less tend to use screens more, but many students don't follow this pattern."

### Why this is Level 5–6:

The question explores a relationship between two variables. Multiple appropriate displays are used. The pattern is described accurately ("weak negative trend") with supporting statistics. The conclusion is evidence-based and acknowledges variation. However, the student doesn't discuss limitations, sample bias, or whether correlation implies causation.

## Annotated Student Response: Levels 7–8

> **Student C's question:** "Is the relationship between sleep duration and screen time different for students aged 12-13 compared to students aged 15-16?"
>
> **Displays:** Two scatter plots (one per age group) with lines of best fit. Box plots comparing screen time distributions for each age group. A summary table with mean, median, IQR, and correlation strength for each group.
>
> **Analysis:** "For younger students (12-13), there is almost no relationship between sleep and screen time (the scatter plot shows no clear trend and the line of best fit is nearly horizontal). For older students (15-16), there is a moderate negative relationship — those sleeping less tend to have higher screen time. The IQR for screen time is also wider in the older group (2.8 hours vs 1.9 hours), suggesting more variation in screen habits as students age."
>
> **Conclusion:** "The relationship between sleep and screen time appears to depend on age. This could be because older students have more autonomy over their screen use and bedtimes. However, my sample only includes 60 students from one school, so these findings may not generalise. The correlation does not prove that screen time CAUSES less sleep — both could be influenced by other factors like homework load or social activities. A larger study tracking the same students over time would provide stronger evidence."

### Why this is Level 7–8:

The question is sophisticated — it doesn't just ask about a relationship, it asks whether that relationship differs between subgroups. The analysis uses multiple appropriate displays and statistical measures, comparing across groups. The pattern description is specific and quantified. The conclusion directly addresses the question with evidence, explicitly discusses causation vs correlation, identifies sample limitations, and proposes how the investigation could be improved. This demonstrates genuine statistical reasoning, not just calculation.

## The Criterion B Progression

| Strand | Levels 1-2 | Levels 3-4 | Levels 5-6 | Levels 7-8 |
|--------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **i. Techniques** | Attempts a basic approach | Uses an appropriate technique | Selects and applies multiple techniques | Selects sophisticated techniques suited to the question |
| **ii. Patterns** | States an obvious observation | Describes a simple pattern | Describes patterns with supporting evidence | Describes complex patterns across subgroups with quantified evidence |
| **iii. Justify** | No justification | Attempts a conclusion | Evidence-based conclusion | Justified conclusion with limitations, alternative explanations, and suggestions for improvement |
