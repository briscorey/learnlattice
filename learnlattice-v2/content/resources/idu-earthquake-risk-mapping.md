## Overview: Why This Works as an IDU

This investigation connects MYP Mathematics (statistics, probability, logarithmic relationships) with MYP Sciences (plate tectonics, seismic waves, geological evidence) through earthquake risk assessment. The interdisciplinary connection is essential — assessing earthquake risk requires both understanding the geological causes AND statistically analysing the historical data to estimate probabilities.

**Mathematics contributes:** Frequency tables and histograms, measures of central tendency, logarithmic scales (the Richter scale itself is logarithmic), probability estimation from historical data, scatter plots of location data, the Gutenberg-Richter frequency-magnitude relationship.

**Science contributes:** Plate tectonic theory, types of plate boundaries and associated earthquake patterns, seismic waves (P-waves, S-waves, surface waves), earthquake measurement and detection, factors affecting earthquake damage (magnitude, depth, distance, building construction).

## Unit Structure (4-5 Lessons)

### Lesson 1: Mapping Historical Earthquakes

Using USGS earthquake catalogue data (earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/search/), students download earthquake data for a specific region (e.g., Pacific Ring of Fire, Mediterranean, or local region) for the past 50 years.

Tasks:
1. Plot earthquake locations on a map (latitude/longitude scatter plot)
2. Colour-code by magnitude: < 4.0 (green), 4.0-5.9 (yellow), 6.0-6.9 (orange), ≥ 7.0 (red)
3. Identify clusters and patterns

**Key discovery:** Earthquakes are not randomly distributed — they cluster along plate boundaries. The mathematical pattern (clustering) reveals the geological structure.

### Lesson 2: Statistical Analysis of Earthquake Data

Students create:
1. A **frequency table** of earthquake magnitudes (grouped: 3.0-3.9, 4.0-4.9, etc.)
2. A **histogram** showing the distribution of magnitudes
3. A **logarithmic frequency plot** (log₁₀ of frequency vs magnitude)

**The Gutenberg-Richter Discovery:** When students plot log(frequency) vs magnitude, the relationship is approximately linear. This means: for every +1 magnitude, frequency decreases by ~10x.

**MYP Maths Criterion B:** Students investigate this pattern, describe the relationship, and use it to predict how often a magnitude 7+ earthquake would occur in their region.

### Lesson 3: Probability and Risk Assessment

Students calculate earthquake probabilities:
1. If there have been 15 magnitude 5+ earthquakes in 50 years, what is the estimated annual probability?
2. What is the probability of at least one magnitude 5+ earthquake in the next 10 years? (Using P = 1 - (1-p)ⁿ)
3. How confident can we be in these estimates? (Discuss sample size and uncertainty)

### Lesson 4: The Building Decision

**MYP Criterion D (both subjects):** Given three potential sites for a new hospital, students must:
1. Analyse the seismic risk at each location (proximity to fault lines, historical earthquake frequency)
2. Consider other factors (soil type affects amplification, building standards, evacuation routes)
3. Recommend a site with full mathematical and scientific justification
4. Acknowledge limitations of their analysis

### Lesson 5 (Extension): Earthquake-Resistant Design

Students research and evaluate earthquake-resistant building techniques:
- Base isolation (rubber bearings that absorb seismic energy)
- Cross-bracing and shear walls
- Tuned mass dampers (like Taipei 101)
- The role of building codes in reducing casualties

## Assessment Pairing

**Mathematics:** Criterion B (Gutenberg-Richter pattern investigation) + Criterion C (statistical communication) + Criterion D (probability-based risk assessment)

**Science:** Criterion A (plate tectonics and seismic wave knowledge) + Criterion B (research question about earthquake patterns) + Criterion D (evaluation of building decisions and societal impacts)

- The colour-coding system for magnitude is intuitive and language-free
- Sentence frames: "Based on the data, a magnitude ___ earthquake occurs approximately once every ___ years in this region. The probability of this occurring in the next 10 years is approximately ___."
