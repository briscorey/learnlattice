## Act 1: The Hook

*Demonstrate:*

Stack 3 plastic cups into a small pyramid (2 on the bottom, 1 on top). Measure the height.

> **The Question:** If you had 100 cups, how tall would the pyramid be? And how many layers before it collapses?

Write your estimate. Do not calculate yet.

---

## Act 2: What Do You Need to Know?

Students build and measure:

| Layers | Cups Used | Total Height (cm) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | ? |
| 2 | 3 | ? |
| 3 | 6 | ? |
| 4 | 10 | ? |
| 5 | 15 | ? |

> **Two patterns to investigate:**
> 1. What is the relationship between layers and height? (Linear — each layer adds the same amount)
> 2. What is the relationship between layers and cups used? (Triangular numbers!)

**Height model:** H = a × layers + b (find a and b from your data)

**Cups model:** Cups = layers × (layers + 1) ÷ 2

---

## Act 3: The Reveal

Build as many layers as possible.

> **When did the model break?** At what point did the tower become unstable? Did the mathematical prediction of height match reality?

---

## The Sequel

- Why does the height model (linear) eventually fail? (Cups compress under weight, base shifts)
- How could you modify the structure to build taller?
- Engineers face this exact problem with real buildings. What are the parallels?
- **Criterion D extension:** Research the world's tallest free-standing structures. What mathematical and engineering principles allow them to stand?

---

## Teaching Notes

**3-Act Structure:** Act 1 hooks with a physical demonstration. Act 2 generates data through hands-on building. Act 3 tests predictions against reality.

**MYP Criterion B Connection:** Students investigate two patterns (height vs. layers, cups vs. layers), describe general rules, and verify them.

**MYP Criterion D Connection:** When the tower collapses, the mathematical model meets reality. Students evaluate why the linear model fails and discuss structural limitations — pure Criterion D at levels 5-8.

**Cross-Disciplinary:** This connects to MYP Sciences (forces, centre of gravity, structural engineering) and could be co-assessed with a science teacher.

**Materials needed:** 50-100 plastic cups per group, rulers, space to build.
