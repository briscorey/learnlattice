## Student Task Sheet

**Subject:** Mathematics | **Criterion:** C (Communicating) | **Time:** 55 minutes

**Instructions:** You will analyse data, create representations, and write about your findings. You will be assessed on how clearly and accurately you communicate mathematical ideas. Rulers and coloured pencils are recommended.

### The Data

A survey asked 30 students in Grade 7: "How many hours of screen time did you have yesterday (not including schoolwork)?"

The responses (in hours) were:

2, 4, 1, 3, 5, 2, 6, 3, 2, 1, 4, 3, 7, 2, 3, 1, 4, 2, 3, 5, 2, 3, 8, 1, 3, 4, 2, 3, 6, 2

### Part A: Organise (Levels 1-4)

1. Organise the data into a frequency table with the following groups: 0-1 hours, 2-3 hours, 4-5 hours, 6+ hours.

2. Calculate the mean, median, and mode of the raw data. Show your working.

### Part B: Represent (Levels 3-6)

3. Create **two different** graphical representations of this data. Choose the types you think are most appropriate.

4. For each graph, include:
   - a clear title
   - labelled axes (with units where appropriate)
   - an appropriate scale
   - accurate plotting of data

5. Write one sentence explaining why you chose each type of graph. What does each one show that the other does not?

### Part C: Analyse and Evaluate (Levels 5-8)

6. A school newspaper writes: "The average Grade 7 student spends 3 hours on screens every day."

   a) Which "average" are they most likely using? Justify your answer.

   b) Is this a fair summary of the data? Explain, referring to the shape of the data distribution.

7. A parent reads the article and says: "That means my child should have exactly 3 hours of screen time."

   Explain at least two reasons why this conclusion is not valid based on the survey data alone. Use mathematical reasoning, not just opinions.


## Task-Specific Rubric

### Levels 1-2
The student attempts a frequency table but it may contain errors. Calculations of mean, median, or mode are attempted with limited accuracy. One graph is drawn but may lack labels, a title, or an appropriate scale.

### Levels 3-4
The student produces a correct frequency table. Mean, median, and mode are calculated with mostly correct working. Two graphs are drawn with titles and labelled axes. The choice of graph is stated but the explanation of why is superficial.

### Levels 5-6
The student selects appropriate graph types and explains what each one reveals. Graphs are fully labelled with accurate scales and correct data. The student identifies which average the newspaper is using and evaluates whether it fairly represents the data, referring to the distribution.

### Levels 7-8
The student discusses the limitations of the survey data with mathematical precision. Evaluates the parent's conclusion by identifying issues such as sample size, self-reporting bias, the difference between a sample statistic and an individual prescription, and the distinction between correlation and causation. Mathematical language is used consistently and correctly throughout.


## Marking Guide

### Part A

**Q1** (2 marks):

| Group | Frequency |
|-------|-----------|
| 0-1 hours | 4 |
| 2-3 hours | 17 |
| 4-5 hours | 6 |
| 6+ hours | 3 |

(1 mark for correct groups, 1 mark for all frequencies correct. Total should be 30.)

**Q2** (4 marks):
- Mean: Sum = 92, Mean = 92/30 = 3.07 (1 mark for correct sum, 1 mark for dividing by 30)
- Median: Data in order, middle values are 15th and 16th = 3 and 3, median = 3 (1 mark)
- Mode: 3 appears 8 times, more than any other value (1 mark)

### Part B

**Q3-4** (6 marks, 3 per graph):
For each graph: correct type (1 mark), all labels and title present (1 mark), data plotted accurately (1 mark).

Appropriate choices include: bar chart (shows frequency of each value), grouped bar chart, pie chart (shows proportions), histogram (shows grouped distribution), dot plot. Accept any reasonable choice if justified.

**Q5** (2 marks): One clear reason per graph explaining what it shows. For example: "I chose a bar chart because it shows the exact frequency of each screen time value. I chose a pie chart because it shows the proportion of students in each group, making it easy to see that more than half the students are in the 2-3 hour range." (1 mark per explanation that goes beyond "it shows the data clearly")

### Part C

**Q6a** (2 marks): They are most likely using the mode or the median (both are 3). The mean is 3.07, which rounds to 3, so they could also be using the mean. (1 mark for identifying which average, 1 mark for justifying with calculation)

**Q6b** (2 marks): The data is slightly skewed right (a few high values like 7 and 8 pull the mean up). The majority of students (17/30) are in the 2-3 hour range, so "3 hours" is a reasonable central tendency, but it hides the spread. Students range from 1 to 8 hours. (1 mark for referencing the distribution, 1 mark for evaluating fairness)

**Q7** (4 marks, 2 marks per valid reason):
Valid reasons include:
- This is a sample of 30 students, not the whole school. The average might be different for a larger group. (sample vs population)
- The survey asks about one day, not typical behaviour. One day could be unusual. (sampling method)
- An average describes a group, not an individual. Telling one child to match the group average is a misuse of statistics. (ecological fallacy)
- The data is self-reported. Students may over- or under-estimate their screen time. (measurement reliability)
- Correlation between screen time and outcomes is not established by this data. (causation vs correlation)

Award 2 marks for each reason that uses mathematical reasoning, 1 mark if the point is valid but vaguely expressed.

**Total: 22 marks**
