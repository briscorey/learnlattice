## Overview

A systematic investigation into the constant term in linear expressions. Students generate sequences, graph them, and compare the results to develop understanding of y-intercept.

## The Investigation

### Stimulus
Present three sequences side by side:
- Sequence A: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, ...
- Sequence B: 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, ...
- Sequence C: −3, −1, 1, 3, 5, ...

**Question**: What is the same? What is different?

### Phase 1 — Tables and Rules
Students create input-output tables for each sequence, find the algebraic rule, and confirm they all have a gradient of 2.

### Phase 2 — Graphing
Plot all three sequences on the same axes. Students observe:
- All lines are parallel (same gradient)
- They cross the y-axis at different points
- The constant in the rule matches where the line starts

### Phase 3 — Generalisation
- "What would y = 2x + 100 look like?"
- "Can you write a rule that starts below the x-axis?"
- "If two lines are parallel, what must be true about their rules?"

## Criterion B Alignment

| Strand | Evidence |
|--------|----------|
| Select a method | Systematic comparison of three related sequences |
| Apply strategies | Use tables, graphing, and algebraic reasoning |
| Describe patterns | Articulate the role of the constant term |
| Verify | Test predictions by graphing new lines |

## EAL Scaffolds
- **Sentence frame**: "When the constant changes from ___ to ___, the graph shifts ___"
- **Visual**: Colour-code each line on the same graph
- **Key vocabulary**: parallel, y-intercept, constant, gradient, shift

## Differentiation
- **Approaching**: Work with positive constants only, use pre-drawn axes
- **Meeting**: All three sequences, own graphs
- **Exceeding**: Investigate what happens when you change the gradient instead
