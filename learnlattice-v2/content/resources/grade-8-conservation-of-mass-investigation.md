## The Hook

Put steel wool on a balance. Note the mass. Now light it with a match and let it burn.

Watch the balance. The number goes **up.**

Burning something made it *heavier*. That should be impossible. Where did the extra mass come from?

---

## The Investigation

### Station 1: Dissolving

1. Weigh a beaker of water (200 mL)
2. Weigh a spoonful of salt separately
3. Add the salt. Stir until dissolved. The salt vanishes.
4. Weigh the solution.

**Result:** The mass of the solution = mass of water + mass of salt. Nothing was lost.

### Station 2: Fizzing (Open vs. Closed)

**Open system:**
1. Weigh a beaker with 100 mL water + an Alka-Seltzer tablet on the side
2. Drop the tablet in. Wait for fizzing to stop.
3. Weigh again.

**Closed system:**
1. Same setup, but inside a sealed zip-lock bag.
2. Weigh before and after.

**Result:** Open system: mass decreases (gas escaped). Closed system: mass stays the same.

### Station 3: Burning Steel Wool

1. Weigh a ball of fine steel wool on a heat-proof mat
2. Hold a match to it. The steel glows and burns.
3. Weigh the residue.

**Result:** The burnt steel wool is **heavier** than the original. Oxygen from the air combined with the iron.

---

## The Science

Antoine Lavoisier established this law in 1789: **in a chemical reaction, the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products.**

Atoms are not created or destroyed. They are rearranged.

Steel wool burning: $4\text{Fe} + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$

The iron atoms are still there. But now oxygen atoms have joined them. that is the extra mass.

Wood burning: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5 + 6\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 6\text{CO}_2 + 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$

The carbon and hydrogen leave as gases. The ash is just the minerals. If you collected ALL the gases, the total mass would equal the original wood + oxygen consumed.

---

## Teaching Notes

The three-station rotation works well in a single lesson. Groups spend 10 minutes at each station. The steel wool demo can be teacher-led if you prefer. it involves fire and needs supervision.

The critical teaching point is the open vs. closed system comparison. Students who understand why mass appears to change in open systems (gas escapes) but is truly conserved (the gas has mass too) have grasped the deepest level of this concept.

For the steel wool, use very fine grade (0000). It catches fire easily with a match. Coarse steel wool will not ignite. The mass increase is typically 1-3 grams. enough to see clearly on a 0.1g balance.
