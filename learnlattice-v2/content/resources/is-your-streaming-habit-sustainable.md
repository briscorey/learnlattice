## The Hook

You watched Netflix for 2 hours last night. You scrolled TikTok for another hour. You listened to Spotify while walking to school.

**How much electricity did all of that use?**

Write your guess on a sticky note. We'll come back to it.

*(Most people guess wrong by a factor of 100. Not because they're bad at guessing — because the energy is invisible.)*

---

## Background: The Invisible Machine

When you press play on a video, here's what actually happens:

1. **The data centre** — a warehouse the size of a football field, filled with thousands of servers, all cooled by massive air conditioning systems — finds your video and sends it as data packets
2. **The network** — fibre optic cables (some running under the ocean), routers, switches, and cell towers relay those packets across potentially thousands of kilometres
3. **Your device** — your phone, laptop, or TV receives the packets, decodes them, and displays the video

Every single stage uses electricity. Your phone's battery drain is the tiny, visible tip of an enormous invisible iceberg.

### The Numbers That Matter

| Activity | Data per hour | Total energy per hour (kWh) | CO₂ per hour (g) |
|----------|--------------|----------------------------|-------------------|
| SD streaming (480p) | 0.7 GB | 0.08 | 36 |
| HD streaming (1080p) | 1.5 GB | 0.17 | 76 |
| 4K streaming (2160p) | 7.0 GB | 0.65 | 290 |
| Music streaming | 0.04 GB | 0.01 | 4 |
| Video call (Zoom) | 0.8 GB | 0.09 | 40 |

*Sources: IEA (2024), Carbon Trust, Shift Project. CO₂ values assume global average grid intensity of ~450g CO₂/kWh.*

## Part A: Your Personal Streaming Audit (Criterion C)

### Step 1: Collect Your Data

For one typical day, record everything you stream:

| Time | Platform | Content | Duration (min) | Quality | Device |
|------|----------|---------|----------------|---------|--------|
| | | | | | |

**Tips:**
- Check your phone's Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing for accurate durations
- Netflix/YouTube settings show your streaming quality
- Don't forget background music streaming!

### Step 2: Calculate Your Daily Energy Footprint

Using the conversion table above, calculate:
- Total data consumed (GB)
- Total energy consumed (kWh)
- Total CO₂ produced (g)

Show your working clearly. Use the calculation chain:

**Hours × Data rate (GB/hr) = Total GB**
**Total GB × Energy factor (kWh/GB) = Total kWh**
**Total kWh × Grid intensity (g CO₂/kWh) = Total CO₂**

### Step 3: Scale It Up

- **Weekly:** Multiply your daily total by 7 (or calculate each day separately if your habits vary)
- **Yearly:** Multiply weekly by 52
- **School-wide:** If there are 800 students at your school with similar habits, what's the total?

### Step 4: Represent Your Findings

Create **at least two** different mathematical representations:
- A **pie chart** showing the breakdown by platform or activity type
- A **bar chart** comparing different streaming qualities (SD vs HD vs 4K)
- A **comparative display** showing your streaming CO₂ alongside other daily activities

## Part B: The Comparison Question (Criterion D)

Your streaming habit produces ___ kg of CO₂ per year.

**But compared to what?**

| Activity | Annual CO₂ (kg) |
|----------|----------------|
| Your streaming (calculate) | ??? |
| Driving 10 km/day | ~1,500 |
| One return flight London–New York | ~1,000 |
| Eating meat daily vs vegetarian | ~600 |
| Charging your phone for a year | ~8 |
| One year of LED lighting (4 hrs/day) | ~20 |

### The Reflection

Using your calculations and comparisons, write a response to this question:

> **"Is your streaming habit an environmental problem worth worrying about?"**

Your response must:
- Use specific numbers from your investigation
- Compare streaming to at least two other activities
- Consider both individual and aggregate impact (you alone vs. all 2+ billion streamers worldwide)
- Acknowledge limitations in your data or calculations
- Arrive at a justified conclusion — not just "yes" or "no"

## Teaching Notes

### Why This Investigation Works

Every student streams. Every student has an opinion about climate change. This investigation connects the two through mathematics, forcing students to replace intuition with calculation. The cognitive conflict between "streaming feels free" and "streaming has a measurable cost" is the engine that drives genuine mathematical thinking.

### Criterion D Excellence

The comparison question in Part B is where Criterion D scores separate. Level 3-4 students will say "streaming is bad." Level 5-6 students will compare numbers. Level 7-8 students will grapple with nuance: individual impact is small, aggregate impact is enormous, and the comparison depends entirely on what you're replacing. A student who concludes "streaming is more sustainable than the alternatives for most entertainment" with mathematical justification is demonstrating sophisticated real-world application.

### Cross-Disciplinary Connections

**Science (Criterion A):** Students explain how data centres work, how electricity is generated, and how CO₂ contributes to greenhouse warming.

**Science (Criterion D):** Students evaluate the social and environmental implications of digital technology — connecting to broader discussions about sustainable development, digital equity (not everyone has the same access to streaming), and corporate responsibility.

### Adaptation for EAL Learners

- Provide the calculation chain as a visual flowchart with worked example
- Pre-teach vocabulary: data centre, carbon footprint, kilowatt-hour, proportion
- Allow students to present Part B as an annotated infographic rather than continuous prose
- Pair EAL students with proficient English speakers for the research phase, but ensure individual calculation work

### Equipment Needed

- Calculator
- Phone/device with Screen Time data
- Graph paper or graphing software
- Access to the data table (provided above — no internet research required for core investigation)
