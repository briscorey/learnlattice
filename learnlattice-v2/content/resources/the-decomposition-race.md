## The Hook

Bury five materials in your school garden:
1. Banana peel
2. Newspaper
3. Plastic bag
4. Leaf
5. Aluminium foil

**Check every week for 6 weeks.** Photograph and weigh what remains.

Which disappears first? Which lasts the longest? And what is doing the disappearing?

---

## The Experiment

### Setup
- Place each item in a mesh bag (so you can retrieve it)
- Bury all five at the same depth in the same soil
- Mark the locations with stakes
- Record initial mass of each item

### Weekly observations
- Carefully dig up each bag
- Photograph the item
- Record changes: colour, texture, smell, visible organisms
- Weigh the remaining material
- Rebury in the same spot

### After 6 weeks
- Plot mass remaining vs time for each material
- Calculate percentage decomposed

## The Science

**Decomposition is not passive decay.** It is an active biological process driven by:
- **Bacteria**. break down simple organic molecules
- **Fungi**. break down complex molecules like cellulose and lignin
- **Invertebrates**. physically break material into smaller pieces

These organisms produce **enzymes** that chemically digest organic materials. They turn dead matter into nutrients that plants absorb. completing the nutrient cycle.

### Why Plastic Resists

Plastic is a synthetic polymer. No organism on Earth evolved to digest it because it did not exist until the 1950s. There are no enzymes that can break the carbon-carbon bonds in polyethylene.

> This is why a plastic bottle can last **450 years** in a landfill. There is nothing alive that can eat it.

---

## Teaching Notes

This is a 6-week longitudinal investigation that teaches patience, data collection over time, and the difference between biodegradable and non-biodegradable materials through direct evidence. The emotional impact of seeing a banana peel vanish while the plastic bag remains unchanged is more powerful than any lecture about pollution.

Start this investigation at the beginning of a unit and observe weekly. The slow reveal builds narrative tension. students genuinely want to know what happened to their buried items.
