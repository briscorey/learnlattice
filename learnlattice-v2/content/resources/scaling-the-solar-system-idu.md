## The Hook

Show a textbook diagram of the solar system — planets neatly spaced, Sun on the left, Neptune on the right.

Ask: **"Is this drawing accurate?"**

Students say yes. Then reveal: **"If the Sun were the size of a basketball, Earth would be the size of a peppercorn — and it would be 26 metres away. Neptune would be over 800 metres away. This diagram is lying to you."**

Ask: **"Can we build the real one?"**

---

## The Investigation

### Part 1: Choosing a Scale

The Sun's diameter is approximately 1,392,000 km. If we make the Sun the size of a basketball (24 cm diameter), our scale factor is:

$$\text{scale factor} = \frac{0.24 \text{ m}}{1{,}392{,}000{,}000 \text{ m}} = 1.72 \times 10^{-10}$$

Students calculate the scaled diameter and distance for each planet:

| Planet | Actual diameter (km) | Actual distance from Sun (million km) |
|--------|---------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Mercury | 4,879 | 57.9 |
| Venus | 12,104 | 108.2 |
| Earth | 12,756 | 149.6 |
| Mars | 6,792 | 227.9 |
| Jupiter | 142,984 | 778.6 |
| Saturn | 120,536 | 1,433.5 |
| Uranus | 51,118 | 2,872.5 |
| Neptune | 49,528 | 4,495.1 |

### Part 2: Building It

Go outside. Place the basketball (Sun) at one end of the school grounds. Walk. Place each planet at its scaled distance.

**The revelation:** Most planets fit within the school grounds. But Neptune will be nearly 800 metres from the Sun. Jupiter is the size of a large marble. Earth is a peppercorn. Mercury is a grain of sand.

Students experience the emptiness of space with their own feet.

### Part 3: Reflection

After the walkout, students write answers to:
1. What surprised you most about the scale model?
2. Why do textbook diagrams show the planets so close together?
3. If you could only tell someone ONE thing about the size of the solar system, what would it be?

---

## Tiered Tasks

### Part A — Criterion C (Levels 1-4)
Calculate the scaled diameter and distance for all 8 planets. Present your results in a clear table with correct units.

### Part B — Criterion B (Levels 3-6)
Is there a pattern in the spacing of planets? Plot distance from Sun against planet number (1-8). Describe the pattern. Is it linear? Exponential? Something else?

### Part C — Criterion D (Levels 5-8)
The nearest star to our Sun (Proxima Centauri) is 4.24 light-years away. Using your scale model, calculate how far away Proxima Centauri would be. Express this distance in a unit that makes sense (metres? kilometres? something else?). Reflect on what this tells us about interstellar travel.

## Teaching Notes

This is an **interdisciplinary unit** connecting MYP Mathematics (proportional reasoning, scientific notation, scale) with MYP Science (solar system, scale of the universe). It can be co-taught or taught in either subject with connections made explicit.

The walkout is essential. Do not skip it. Students who calculate the distances on paper will forget them in a week. Students who walk 800 metres to place Neptune will remember it for years.

**EAL scaffold:** The walkout requires no English. Give each student a planet card with the name, a photo, and the scaled distance. They walk and place. The mathematics speaks through their feet.
