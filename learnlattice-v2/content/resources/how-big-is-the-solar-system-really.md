## The Hook

Every diagram of the solar system is a lie.

Not a small lie. A massive, fundamental lie. In textbook diagrams, the planets are shown close together, almost touching. In reality, the solar system is **almost entirely empty space**.

**Your job:** Build a scale model. Use real maths. See if it fits in your school.

---

## The Calculation

If the Sun is a beach ball (30cm diameter), calculate the scale factor:

$$\text{Scale} = \frac{0.3 \text{ m}}{1,391,000,000 \text{ m}} = 1 : 4,637,000,000$$

Now calculate the size and distance of each planet:

| Planet | Real diameter | Model size | Real distance from Sun | Model distance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mercury | 4,879 km | ? mm | 58 million km | ? m |
| Venus | 12,104 km | ? mm | 108 million km | ? m |
| Earth | 12,742 km | ? mm | 150 million km | ? m |
| Mars | 6,779 km | ? mm | 228 million km | ? m |
| Jupiter | 139,820 km | ? mm | 778 million km | ? m |
| Saturn | 116,460 km | ? mm | 1,434 million km | ? m |

### The Shock

At this scale, **Earth is a tiny grain of sand, 32 metres from the beach ball Sun**. Jupiter is a marble, 168 metres away. Saturn is nearly 310 metres. Neptune? **Over a kilometre.**

Can your school even fit this model?

---

## The Walk

Take the class outside. Place the Sun (beach ball) at one end of the school grounds. Walk the distances. Place each planet.

Most schools run out of space around Jupiter. That IS the lesson.

---

## Teaching Notes

This investigation makes the abstract concrete in a way that no diagram, video, or lecture ever can. The physical act of walking 32 metres from the Sun to Earth. and realising that Earth is a speck of sand. creates a visceral understanding of cosmic scale.

The calculation practice is real mathematics: division, unit conversion, and proportional reasoning. But it serves a scientific purpose, which makes it meaningful rather than mechanical.
