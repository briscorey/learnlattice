## Student Task Sheet

**Subject:** Mathematics | **Criterion:** A (Knowing and Understanding) | **Time:** 50 minutes

**Instructions:** Answer all questions. Show your working clearly. A calculator may be used for Questions 5 and 6 only.

### Question 1 (Levels 1-2)

Convert each of the following:

a) Write 3/4 as a decimal.

b) Write 0.6 as a fraction in simplest form.

c) Write 45% as a decimal.

d) Write 1/5 as a percentage.

### Question 2 (Levels 3-4)

Place the following numbers in order from smallest to largest:

$$0.35, \quad \frac{1}{3}, \quad 30\%, \quad \frac{3}{8}$$

Show your method clearly.

### Question 3 (Levels 3-4)

A student says: "1/3 is the same as 0.3 because you just remove the fraction bar."

Is this correct? Explain your reasoning using a calculation.

### Question 4 (Levels 5-6)

A shop offers 25% off all items. Mia buys a jacket originally priced at ¥480.

a) Calculate how much Mia pays after the discount.

b) The shop then adds a 10% surcharge for gift wrapping on the discounted price. What is Mia's total cost?

c) Mia's friend says the combined effect is the same as a 15% discount on the original price. Is this correct? Justify your answer with calculations.

### Question 5 (Levels 5-6)

Complete the table by filling in the missing values. Simplify fractions where possible.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|----------|---------|------------|
| 5/8 | | |
| | 0.125 | |
| | | 66.7% |

### Question 6 (Levels 7-8)

A bacteria population doubles every hour. After 1 hour, the population has increased by 100%.

a) After 2 hours, a student writes: "The population has increased by 200%." Is this correct? Explain why or why not, using actual calculations.

b) After n hours, write an expression for the percentage increase from the original population. Explain your reasoning.


## Task-Specific Rubric

### Levels 1-2
The student converts between fractions, decimals, and percentages in straightforward cases. Some errors may be present. Limited or no working shown.

### Levels 3-4
The student converts accurately in routine cases. Orders rational numbers by converting to a common form. Identifies errors in mathematical reasoning with partial explanation.

### Levels 5-6
The student applies fraction, decimal, and percentage knowledge to multi-step problems. Justifies whether claims are correct using calculations, not just stating yes or no. Completes conversion tables including recurring decimals.

### Levels 7-8
The student analyses compound percentage problems and explains why intuitive reasoning fails. Generalises patterns using algebraic expressions. Demonstrates understanding of exponential growth contexts involving percentages.


## Marking Guide

### Question 1 (4 marks)
a) 0.75 (1 mark)
b) 3/5 (1 mark)
c) 0.45 (1 mark)
d) 20% (1 mark)

### Question 2 (3 marks)
Convert all to decimals: 0.35, 0.333..., 0.30, 0.375 (1 mark for method)
Order: 30%, 1/3, 0.35, 3/8 (1 mark)
Clear working showing the conversion process (1 mark)

### Question 3 (2 marks)
No, it is incorrect (1 mark). 1/3 = 0.333... (recurring), not 0.3. The student can show that 0.3 = 3/10, which is not equal to 1/3 (1 mark for mathematical justification).

### Question 4 (6 marks)
a) 25% of 480 = 120. Mia pays 480 - 120 = ¥360 (2 marks)
b) 10% of 360 = 36. Total = 360 + 36 = ¥396 (2 marks)
c) 15% of 480 = 72. A 15% discount would give 480 - 72 = ¥408. Mia actually paid ¥396. So the friend is incorrect. The 10% surcharge applies to the already-discounted price, not the original. (2 marks: 1 for calculation, 1 for clear justification)

### Question 5 (3 marks)
Row 1: 5/8 = 0.625 = 62.5% (1 mark)
Row 2: 0.125 = 1/8 = 12.5% (1 mark)
Row 3: 66.7% = 0.667 = 2/3 (1 mark, accept 667/1000 but award full marks for recognising 2/3)

### Question 6 (4 marks)
a) After 2 hours: original x 2 x 2 = 4 times original. Increase = 3 times original = 300% increase, not 200%. The student is wrong because doubling happens to the new population each time, not the original. (2 marks)
b) After n hours: population = original x 2^n. Percentage increase = (2^n - 1) x 100%. (2 marks: 1 for expression, 1 for explanation)

**Total: 22 marks**
