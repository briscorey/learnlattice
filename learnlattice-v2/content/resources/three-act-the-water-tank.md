## Act 1: The Hook

*Show or describe this scenario:*

A garden tap is running into a large rectangular water tank. After 10 seconds, the water level has risen by 2 cm. The tank is 80 cm tall.

> **The Question:** How long will it take to fill the tank completely?

Write your estimate on your whiteboard. Do NOT calculate yet — this is your gut feeling.

---

## Act 2: What Do You Need to Know?

Students request information. Reveal only what they ask for:

- The tank is 80 cm tall, 60 cm wide, 40 cm deep.
- The tap fills 2 cm of height in 10 seconds.
- The flow rate is constant.
- The tank was empty at the start.

> **Solve it.** Show your method clearly. What assumptions are you making?

**Direct approach:** 80 cm ÷ 2 cm per 10 seconds = 40 intervals × 10 seconds = **400 seconds ≈ 6 minutes 40 seconds.**

**Rate approach:** Flow rate = 60 × 40 × 2 = 4,800 cm³ per 10 seconds = 480 cm³/s. Tank volume = 60 × 40 × 80 = 192,000 cm³. Time = 192,000 ÷ 480 = **400 seconds.**

---

## Act 3: The Reveal

The tank fills in approximately **400 seconds (6 minutes 40 seconds)** under ideal conditions.

> **But wait —** in reality, would it take exactly this long? What could change?

---

## The Sequel: When Does the Model Break?

This is where Criterion D comes alive. Discuss:

- What if the water pressure drops as the tank fills? (It often does in real systems.)
- What if the tank is not perfectly rectangular? (Most real tanks taper.)
- What if someone is simultaneously using water inside the house?
- How much water does this use? At what cost? Is it sustainable?

> **Extension:** If your school has a water tank, measure the actual fill rate. Compare your prediction to reality. What explains the difference?

---

## Teaching Notes

**3-Act Structure:** Act 1 creates intellectual need (estimation). Act 2 gives students agency over what information they request. Act 3 validates and then complicates.

**MYP Criterion D Connection:** The task requires students to apply proportional reasoning in a real-world context and evaluate the limitations of their mathematical model. The sequel questions push toward levels 7-8 by asking students to reflect on model accuracy.

**MYP Criterion C Connection:** The two solution methods (direct proportional reasoning vs. volume rate calculation) provide an opportunity to discuss which communication approach is clearer.

**Global Context — Sustainability:** Connect to water usage data. How much water does a household use daily? How does this compare globally? This turns a rate problem into a genuine sustainability investigation.

**EAL Scaffold:** The visual nature of a filling tank is universally understandable. Provide sentence frames: "I estimate ___ because ___" and "My calculation assumes that ___".
