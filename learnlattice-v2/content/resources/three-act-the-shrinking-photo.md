## Act 1: The Hook

A student is making a poster. They have a photo that is 2400 × 1800 pixels (a 4:3 ratio). They need to resize it to fit a display that is 1920 × 1080 pixels (a 16:9 ratio).

They just drag the corners to fit. The photo looks **stretched and distorted**.

> **The Question:** What went wrong? And what is the correct way to resize the photo?

---

## Act 2: The Mathematics of Aspect Ratio

**The original photo:** 2400 × 1800. Ratio = 2400:1800 = 4:3

**The display:** 1920 × 1080. Ratio = 1920:1080 = 16:9

These ratios are NOT the same. 4:3 ≠ 16:9 (because 4/3 ≈ 1.33 but 16/9 ≈ 1.78).

> **Three options to investigate:**
>
> **Option A — Stretch to fit:** Change both dimensions independently. Width scale factor = 1920/2400 = 0.8. Height scale factor = 1080/1800 = 0.6. Different scale factors = distortion.
>
> **Option B — Fit width, crop height:** Scale width to 1920 (factor = 0.8). New height = 1800 × 0.8 = 1440. Crop 1440 to 1080 (lose 360 pixels = 25% of the image).
>
> **Option C — Fit height, add bars:** Scale height to 1080 (factor = 0.6). New width = 2400 × 0.6 = 1440. Display width is 1920, so add black bars (240 pixels each side).

Which option preserves the image best? Which loses the most information?

---

## Act 3: The Reveal

Professional image editing always uses **uniform scale factors** — the same multiplier for width and height. This preserves the aspect ratio and prevents distortion.

> **The rule:** If the scale factor for width ≠ the scale factor for height, the image distorts. Proportional reasoning tells us when this will happen.

---

## The Sequel

- What aspect ratio would a photo need to have to fit perfectly on a 16:9 screen with no cropping and no bars?
- Movie screens use ratios like 2.39:1 (cinemascope). How much of a 16:9 TV is wasted when showing a cinemascope film?
- Social media platforms use different ratios: Instagram (1:1), TikTok (9:16), YouTube (16:9). Design a poster that works on all three without important content being cropped. What is the "safe zone"?
- **Extension:** Why do old TV shows look different on modern TVs? (4:3 content on 16:9 screens)

## Teaching Notes

**3-Act Structure:** Act 1 presents a relatable problem (everyone has experienced photo distortion). Act 2 applies ratio and proportion to solve it. Act 3 reveals the mathematical principle.

**MYP Criterion D Connection:** The task applies mathematical reasoning (ratio, proportion, scale factor) to a genuine digital media problem. Students evaluate three different solutions and discuss trade-offs.

**MYP Criterion C Connection:** Communicating the difference between the three options requires clear mathematical explanation with supporting calculations.

**Global Context — Personal and Cultural Expression:** Digital media, photography, and visual communication are central to students' lives. This task connects mathematics to their everyday experience of screens, social media, and image editing.

**EAL Scaffold:** Visual demonstration is essential — show actual distorted vs. proportional images side by side. The mathematics of ratio is accessible through visual comparison before any calculation.
