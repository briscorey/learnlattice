## The Hook

Imagine a rope that fits perfectly around the Earth's equator — about 40,000 km long.

Now add just **1 metre** of extra rope and redistribute it evenly so the rope floats the same height everywhere.

**How high off the ground is the rope now?**

> **Before you calculate, GUESS.** Write your prediction on your whiteboard. Most people guess less than a millimetre.

---

## The Algebra

Let the Earth's radius = R (about 6,371 km, but it does not matter).

Original rope = 2πR

New rope = 2πR + 1 metre

New rope also = 2π(R + h), where h is the height above ground.

So: 2π(R + h) = 2πR + 1

2πR + 2πh = 2πR + 1

2πh = 1

**h = 1/(2π) ≈ 0.159 metres ≈ about 16 cm**

> **The rope floats 16 cm off the ground.** Adding just 1 metre to a 40,000 km rope lifts it high enough to slide a hand underneath — everywhere around the entire planet.

---

## Why This Is Shocking

Our intuition says 1 metre is nothing compared to 40,000 km. But the algebra shows that the height depends **only on the extra rope length**, not on the radius. The same 1 metre of extra rope around a basketball, a football field, Jupiter, or the Sun would all give the same 16 cm gap.

> **This is why algebra matters.** Our intuition about scale is unreliable. The formula reveals a truth we could never have guessed.

---

## Teaching Notes

**BTC Practices Used:** Prediction before calculation (engages intuition that the algebra will contradict), random groups, vertical non-permanent surfaces.

**MYP Criterion B Connection:** The investigation leads to the discovery that height is independent of radius — a genuinely surprising pattern that demands justification.

**MYP Criterion D Connection:** This is a real-world application where the mathematics contradicts everyday intuition. Discussion: "When should we trust our mathematical models over our gut feelings?"

**Key Moment:** Collect predictions before anyone calculates. The gap between prediction (~0.001 mm) and reality (~16 cm) creates intellectual need for the algebraic explanation.
