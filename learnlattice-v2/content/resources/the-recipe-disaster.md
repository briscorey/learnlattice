## The Hook

Here is a pancake recipe for 4 people:

- 1½ cups flour
- 2 eggs
- ¾ cup milk
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 1 tablespoon butter

**Scale it for 10 people.** Every number changes. Some become fractions. Some become mixed numbers. Welcome to the real reason fractions exist.

---

## The Calculation

Scale factor: 10/4 = 5/2 = 2.5

| Ingredient | For 4 | × 2.5 | For 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flour | 1½ cups | 1.5 × 2.5 | 3¾ cups |
| Eggs | 2 | 2 × 2.5 | 5 |
| Milk | ¾ cup | 0.75 × 2.5 | 1⅞ cups |
| Sugar | 2 tbsp | 2 × 2.5 | 5 tbsp |
| Butter | 1 tbsp | 1 × 2.5 | 2½ tbsp |

> **Try this:** Scale the recipe for 7 people. The scale factor is 7/4. Now every calculation involves fraction multiplication.

---

## The Challenge Rounds

1. **Scale for 1 person** (divide everything by 4)
2. **Scale for 15 people** (multiply by 15/4)
3. **Scale for 100 people** — a school event!
4. **You only have 2 cups of flour** — what is the maximum number of people you can serve?

Round 4 is the hardest: you need to work **backwards** from a constraint.

---

## Teaching Notes

This is fraction arithmetic with a genuine purpose. Students who groan at "multiply 3/4 by 5/2" will happily calculate it when the answer determines how much milk they need for a real recipe.

If your school has a kitchen, actually make the pancakes. Nothing teaches proportional reasoning like serving 10 people from a recipe designed for 4 — and discovering that 3/4 cup scaled by 2.5 really does produce the right amount of batter.
