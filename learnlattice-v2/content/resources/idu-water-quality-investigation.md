## Overview: Why This Works as an IDU

This investigation connects MYP Mathematics (statistics, data analysis) with MYP Sciences (chemistry, ecology) through a genuine shared context: evaluating water quality. The interdisciplinary connection is authentic — you cannot determine whether water is safe without both scientific measurement AND statistical analysis. Neither discipline alone answers the question.

**Mathematics contributes:** Data collection design, calculation of measures of central tendency and spread, graphical representation, statistical comparison to standard values, evaluation of data reliability.

**Science contributes:** Understanding of pH, dissolved solids, turbidity as indicators of water quality, knowledge of contamination sources, ecological implications of water quality, experimental methodology.

---

## Unit Structure (4-5 Lessons)

### Lesson 1: The Question and the Design

**Opening (10 min):** Present the hook — rank water sources from safest to least safe without any data. Collect predictions visually (each group puts their ranking on a vertical whiteboard). Discuss: what would you need to MEASURE to check your ranking?

**Core (30 min):** Introduce the water quality indicators students will measure:
- **pH** (acidity/alkalinity) — WHO guideline: 6.5–8.5
- **Turbidity** (cloudiness) — WHO guideline: < 4 NTU
- **Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)** — WHO guideline: < 600 mg/L
- **Temperature** — affects dissolved oxygen and biological activity

Students design their data collection plan: How many samples from each source? How will they ensure fair testing? What equipment do they need? How will they record data?

**MYP Science Criterion B (Inquiring and Designing):** Students formulate a research question, identify variables, and design a method for collecting sufficient, relevant data.

**MYP Maths Criterion B (Investigating Patterns):** Students decide how many measurements are needed per source to calculate reliable statistics (at least 5 per source for meaningful measures of spread).

### Lesson 2: Data Collection (Science Focus)

**Practical session:** Students collect water samples from 3-5 sources (school tap water, filtered water, bottled water, nearby pond/river if accessible, rainwater collection). Each group tests each sample using pH strips/meters, TDS meters, and turbidity tubes.

**Key teaching move:** Insist on at least 5 independent measurements per source per indicator. Students will later need this data for statistical analysis — if they only take one measurement, the maths becomes trivial.

**Record data in a shared class spreadsheet** so all groups can access all data for analysis.

### Lesson 3: Statistical Analysis (Mathematics Focus)

Students analyse the collected data using statistical methods:

1. Calculate the **mean** and **standard deviation** for each indicator at each water source
2. Create **box plots or bar charts with error bars** comparing water sources
3. Determine whether each source meets WHO guidelines (using the mean ± 1 standard deviation range)
4. Identify any **outliers** and discuss whether to include or exclude them

**MYP Maths Criterion C (Communicating):** Students must present their statistical analysis clearly — labelled graphs, appropriate scales, correct use of statistical terminology.

**Key distinction for levels 7-8:** Students who explain WHY they chose a particular graph type (e.g., "I used box plots because they show both the central tendency and the spread, allowing direct comparison of variability between sources") demonstrate higher-level communication than students who simply produce a correct graph.

### Lesson 4: Interpretation and Evaluation

Students write a combined analysis addressing:

1. **Which water sources meet WHO safety standards?** (Use statistical evidence, not single measurements)
2. **How reliable is our data?** (Consider sample size, measurement uncertainty, and sources of error)
3. **What are the ecological and health implications?** (Connect to science knowledge about contamination, treatment, access)
4. **What would you recommend?** (Evidence-based conclusion about water safety at your school/community)

**MYP Maths Criterion D (Applying Mathematics):** Students apply statistical analysis to evaluate a real-world question and discuss the limitations of their mathematical model.

**MYP Science Criterion D (Reflecting on Impacts):** Students evaluate the implications of water quality for health, environment, and equity — who has access to safe water, and who does not?

### Lesson 5 (Extension): Presentation and Action

Groups present findings to the class or school community. Possible actions: write to the school administration about water quality, create infographics for younger students, compare findings to global water access data.

## Assessment Design

This IDU can be assessed collaboratively by the mathematics and science teachers. Recommended criterion pairings:

**Mathematics assessment:** Criterion B (pattern investigation in the data) + Criterion C (statistical communication) + Criterion D (real-world application and model evaluation)

**Science assessment:** Criterion B (experimental design) + Criterion C (data processing and evaluation) + Criterion D (reflection on health and environmental impacts)

A single student report can be assessed against both sets of criteria, with each teacher focusing on their discipline's strands.

## Materials Needed

- pH strips or digital pH meter
- TDS meter (available for ~$10-15 online)
- Turbidity tube or Secchi disc
- Thermometer
- Sample collection bottles (clean, labelled)
- Shared spreadsheet (Google Sheets works well)
- WHO drinking water quality guidelines (provided as a handout)

---

## EAL Scaffolds

- Provide a bilingual glossary: pH, turbidity, dissolved solids, contamination, mean, standard deviation, outlier
- Use sentence frames for the analysis: "The mean pH of [source] was ___, which is [within/outside] the WHO guideline of ___. This suggests that ___."
- The practical data collection is accessible regardless of language level — students can measure, record, and graph before writing
- Pair EAL students with fluent English speakers during the analysis phase
