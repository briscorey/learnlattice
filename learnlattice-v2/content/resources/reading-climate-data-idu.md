## The Hook

Write on the board: **"The Earth has warmed by 1.1°C."**

Ask: **"Is that a lot or a little?"**

Students will almost universally say "a little." Then show:
- The difference between an ice age and today is approximately 5°C
- A 2°C rise is projected to cause the collapse of coral reefs, food system disruption, and the displacement of hundreds of millions of people
- The last time the Earth was 3°C warmer, sea levels were 15-25 metres higher

Ask again: **"Is 1.1°C a lot or a little?"**

The investigation is about learning to read data carefully enough to answer this question with evidence.

---

## The Investigation

### Working with Real Data

Students analyse NASA GISS global temperature anomaly data (publicly available at data.giss.nasa.gov). The data shows yearly temperature anomalies (difference from a baseline average) from 1880 to present.

### Part 1: Read the Data

Plot the temperature anomaly for each decade from 1880 to 2020. Calculate the mean anomaly for each decade.

### Part 2: Find the Trend

1. Draw a line of best fit through the decade averages
2. Calculate the average rate of warming per decade
3. Is the rate of warming constant, or is it accelerating?

### Part 3: Interpret

1. Calculate the mean and standard deviation of the anomalies for 1880-1920 (pre-industrial baseline)
2. How many standard deviations above this baseline is the current anomaly?
3. In statistical terms, what does this tell us about whether the current warming is within natural variation?

---

## Tiered Tasks

### Part A — Criterion C (Levels 1-4)

1. Create a clear, labelled line graph of decade-average temperature anomalies from 1880 to 2020
2. Add a title, axis labels with units, and a line of best fit
3. Describe the overall trend in 2-3 sentences

### Part B — Criterion B (Levels 3-6)

1. Calculate the rate of warming for three periods: 1880-1940, 1940-1980, 1980-2020
2. Is the rate of warming increasing, decreasing, or staying the same?
3. If the current rate continues, predict the anomaly in 2050. State your assumption.

### Part C — Criterion D (Levels 5-8)

A climate sceptic says: "The climate has always changed. There's nothing unusual about the current warming."

Using your statistical analysis:
1. Calculate whether the current rate of warming is within the range of natural variability shown in the pre-industrial data
2. Evaluate the claim using evidence. Is the current warming unusual in statistical terms?
3. Discuss: what is the difference between "the climate has always changed" and "the current rate of change is unprecedented"? Why does the distinction matter?

## Teaching Notes

This is an **interdisciplinary unit** connecting MYP Mathematics (statistics, data analysis, line of best fit, standard deviation) with MYP Science (climate systems, Earth science, evidence-based reasoning).

**Important pedagogical note:** This investigation teaches students to read data, not to form political opinions. The statistical analysis is the learning goal. The data speaks for itself — students who engage honestly with the numbers will reach evidence-based conclusions regardless of prior beliefs.

**EAL scaffold:** The graph is visual. The trend is visible. Numbers are universal. Students who cannot follow a verbal explanation of climate change can still plot data, calculate means, and draw trend lines. The mathematics is the entry point.
