## The Hook

You have two jugs: one holds **5 litres** and one holds **3 litres**. Neither has any markings.

You need to measure **exactly 4 litres** of water. You can only fill, empty, or pour from one jug to the other.

> **Work at your whiteboard.** Track the amount in each jug after every step.

---

## Track Your Steps

| Step | Action | 5L Jug | 3L Jug |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | Both empty | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | Fill 5L jug | 5 | 0 |
| 2 | Pour into 3L | 2 | 3 |
| 3 | Empty 3L jug | 2 | 0 |
| 4 | Pour 5L into 3L | 0 | 2 |
| 5 | Fill 5L jug | 5 | 2 |
| 6 | Pour into 3L (only 1L fits) | **4** | 3 |

> **Done in 6 steps.** Can you find a different route? A shorter one?

---

## Extend the Thinking

- Can you measure **every** amount from 1 to 8 litres using these two jugs?
- What amounts are possible with a **7-litre** and a **4-litre** jug?
- Is there a mathematical relationship between the jug sizes and the amounts you can measure?
- **For advanced students:** You can measure exactly 1 litre whenever the jug sizes are **coprime** (share no common factor other than 1). Can you explain why?

---

## Teaching Notes

**BTC Practices Used:** Thinking task with accessible entry, random groups, vertical non-permanent surfaces, oral delivery (do not write the problem down — tell it as a story).

**MYP Criterion C Connection:** The step-by-step tracking table requires precise communication. Students must describe each action unambiguously.

**Key Pedagogical Move:** After students solve the 4-litre problem, ask: "Can you measure exactly 1 litre?" This extends the investigation and connects to the mathematical theory of greatest common divisors.

**EAL Scaffold:** Demonstrate the first two steps with actual containers or drawings. The visual/physical nature of the task makes it accessible regardless of language level.
