## The Hook

Show three images: a honeycomb, Islamic geometric art from the Alhambra, and an M.C. Escher print.

Ask: **"What do these three things have in common?"**

They all tessellate. shapes that fit together perfectly with no gaps and no overlaps.

Then ask: **"Which regular polygons tessellate? Why can a hexagon tile a floor but an octagon can't?"**

---

## Core Concept: Tessellation and Interior Angles

A regular polygon tessellates if its interior angle divides exactly into 360°.

$$\text{Interior angle} = \frac{(n-2) \times 180°}{n}$$

- Triangle: \\(\frac{180°}{3} = 60°\\). Does \\(60° \times 6 = 360°\\)? Yes. **Tessellates.**
- Square: \\(\frac{360°}{4} = 90°\\). Does \\(90° \times 4 = 360°\\)? Yes. **Tessellates.**
- Pentagon: \\(\frac{540°}{5} = 108°\\). Does \\(108°\\) divide into \\(360°\\)? No. **Does not tessellate.**
- Hexagon: \\(\frac{720°}{6} = 120°\\). Does \\(120° \times 3 = 360°\\)? Yes. **Tessellates.**

Only THREE regular polygons tessellate: triangle, square, hexagon. This is a beautiful result. and students can discover it themselves.

## Tiered Tasks

### Part A. Criterion C (Levels 1-4)
1. Calculate the interior angle of every regular polygon from triangle (3 sides) to dodecagon (12 sides).
2. For each, test whether the angle divides into 360°. Record in a table.
3. Which regular polygons tessellate? Create a poster showing all three tessellation patterns.

### Part B. Criterion B (Levels 3-6)
1. Semi-regular tessellations use TWO or more different regular polygons. The rule: the angles meeting at each vertex must sum to exactly 360°.
2. Find at least two combinations that work (e.g., octagons + squares: 135° + 135° + 90° = 360°).
3. State a general rule: what must be true about the angles at every vertex for a tessellation to work?

### Part C. Criterion B (Levels 5-8)
1. Explain mathematically why the hexagon is the most efficient shape for covering a surface (hint: it has the smallest perimeter for a given area of any tessellating polygon). This is the answer to "why do bees build hexagons?"
2. Escher created tessellations from irregular shapes by modifying regular polygons. Starting with a square, show how translating a cut from one side to the opposite side creates a new tessellating shape. Prove that your shape tessellates.

---

## Teaching Notes

Give students physical polygon tiles if possible. pattern blocks work perfectly. Let them discover which shapes tessellate by TRYING, not by calculating. The calculation comes after to explain what they already found. This is inquiry: experience first, formalise second.

The Escher connection is powerful for students who think mathematics isn't creative. Creating their own tessellating design. colouring it, turning it into art. produces work they're proud of, and it's genuinely mathematical.
