## MYP Assessment Made Simple

Assessment in the IB Middle Years Programme can feel overwhelming — four criteria, eight achievement levels, task-specific clarifications, and moderation expectations. The LearnLattice Assessment Toolkit breaks it down into tools you can actually use in your classroom this week.

### What You Will Find Here

This toolkit includes rubric reference cards for all four MYP criteria ([A](/assessment/criterion-a/), [B](/assessment/criterion-b/), [C](/assessment/criterion-c/), [D](/assessment/criterion-d/)), each with plain-language descriptors and example student phrases at every level. We also provide annotated student exemplars showing real work at levels 3-4, 5-6, and 7-8, a comment bank for writing criterion-referenced feedback, and a [complete guide to MYP moderation](/blog/complete-guide-myp-moderation/).

### Why This Matters

Most MYP teachers spend hours writing assessment tasks, grading student work, and then second-guessing whether their judgement aligns with the IB standard. These tools save that time. Print a rubric card for your desk. Open an exemplar during moderation. Copy a comment from the bank and adapt it. The goal is assessment that is accurate, efficient, and consistent across your department.

### For New MYP Teachers

If you are new to criterion-referenced assessment, start with our blog posts: [Five Criterion B Mistakes](/blog/five-criterion-b-mistakes/) explains the most common errors in the hardest criterion, and [What Level 7 Actually Looks Like](/blog/what-level-7-actually-looks-like/) shows the real gap between "good" and "excellent" student work.
