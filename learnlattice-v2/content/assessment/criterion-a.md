## What Is MYP Criterion A?

Criterion A assesses whether students can demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the concepts and skills they have studied. In mathematics, this means selecting and applying the right techniques, using correct notation, and solving problems in both familiar and unfamiliar situations. In science, it means explaining scientific knowledge, applying concepts to new scenarios, and analysing information to make scientifically supported judgements.

This is often the criterion that feels most like a "test" — but the IB expects more than recall. Even at the lower levels, students need to apply knowledge, not just state it. At the highest levels, students must transfer understanding to contexts they have never seen before.

## How Criterion A Differs Across Subjects

In **MYP Mathematics**, Criterion A focuses on whether students can select appropriate techniques, apply them correctly, and solve problems in both familiar and unfamiliar situations. A student at level 7-8 does not just get the right answer — they choose the most efficient method, use precise mathematical language, and explain their reasoning.

In **MYP Science**, Criterion A assesses whether students can explain scientific knowledge, apply it to make predictions, and analyse information using scientific reasoning. At the highest levels, students are expected to evaluate the quality of evidence and make judgements that are scientifically supported.

## Common Misconceptions About Criterion A

Many teachers treat Criterion A as a straightforward knowledge test. The IB descriptors, however, require application at every level. A student who memorises a formula but cannot apply it to a new problem has not demonstrated Criterion A at any meaningful level.

Another common mistake is assessing Criterion A only through written tests. The IB allows assessment through any task that lets students demonstrate knowledge and understanding — including investigations, presentations, and practical work.
