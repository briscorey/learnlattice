## What Is MYP Criterion D?

Criterion D asks students to take the mathematics or science they have learned and apply it to authentic, real-world situations. In mathematics, this means identifying the relevant mathematical elements in a real problem, selecting an appropriate strategy, arriving at a solution, and then reflecting on whether the answer actually makes sense in context.

This is the criterion where students must think like practitioners, not just like students. A mathematically correct answer that is unreasonable in context (like calculating that a person is 0.3 metres tall) should be flagged as a problem, not presented as a solution.

## The Difference Between Criterion B and Criterion D

Teachers often confuse Criterion B and Criterion D. The simplest distinction: Criterion B starts with a pattern and asks "what is the rule?" Criterion D starts with a real situation and asks "what mathematics helps here?" In Criterion B, the mathematics is the point. In Criterion D, the mathematics is a tool for understanding something about the world.

A student working at level 7-8 in Criterion D does not just solve the problem. They evaluate whether their mathematical model is appropriate for the situation, discuss its limitations, and consider what would happen if assumptions changed. This reflective step is what separates competent from excellent.

## Writing Effective Criterion D Tasks

The best Criterion D tasks use genuinely messy real-world data. not textbook problems dressed up with a context sentence. A strong task might ask students to plan a budget using real prices, optimise a design using measurements they take themselves, or analyse real data to make a recommendation. The context should matter: if you could remove the real-world framing and the task would be unchanged, it is not a genuine Criterion D task.
