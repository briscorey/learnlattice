## What MYP Sciences Criterion A Actually Assesses

Criterion A in MYP Sciences. Knowing and Understanding. assesses whether students can recall scientific knowledge, apply their understanding to solve problems, and analyse information to make scientifically supported judgements. While it shares a name with the mathematics version, the science Criterion A has a distinct emphasis: students must demonstrate understanding of scientific concepts, models, and theories, and use scientific knowledge to analyse problems and interpret information.

The critical distinction from traditional science testing is the expectation that students *apply* knowledge to new situations, not merely reproduce it from memory. A student who can recite the definition of density but cannot use it to predict whether an object will float has not demonstrated Criterion A at levels 5-6 or above.

## The Achievement Levels in Plain Language

**Levels 1-2 (Limited):** The student recalls some scientific facts and definitions when prompted. Attempts to apply scientific knowledge but with significant errors. May identify a scientific concept but cannot explain it.

**Levels 3-4 (Adequate):** The student describes scientific knowledge correctly. Applies understanding to solve familiar problems. Analyses information and makes basic judgements. May struggle when the context is unfamiliar.

**Levels 5-6 (Substantial):** The student describes and explains scientific knowledge. Applies understanding to solve problems in familiar and unfamiliar situations. Analyses and evaluates information using scientific reasoning.

**Levels 7-8 (Excellent):** The student explains scientific knowledge with precision and depth. Applies understanding to solve complex problems across multiple contexts. Analyses and evaluates information to make scientifically supported judgements, considering the quality and reliability of evidence.

### The Critical Jump: Describing vs. Explaining

At levels 3-4, a student *describes* photosynthesis: "Plants use sunlight to make food." At levels 5-6, a student *explains* it: "Chlorophyll in the leaf absorbs light energy, which drives a chemical reaction converting carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen." At levels 7-8, a student *evaluates*: "The simplified equation 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ obscures the complexity of the light-dependent and light-independent reactions, and does not account for factors like light intensity and temperature that limit the rate in real conditions."

## Designing Criterion A Tasks That Go Beyond Recall

The most common mistake is creating tests that only assess the lowest levels. A task that asks "Define osmosis" and "Name the parts of a cell" caps most students at levels 3-4 regardless of their actual understanding. Genuine Criterion A assessment includes:

**Knowledge recall** (levels 1-4): Define terms, label diagrams, state scientific facts.

**Application** (levels 3-6): Use scientific knowledge to solve problems in familiar contexts, then in unfamiliar contexts. Example: "A student puts a wilted lettuce leaf in pure water. Using your knowledge of osmosis, explain what will happen and why."

**Analysis and evaluation** (levels 5-8): Interpret data, evaluate claims, make evidence-based judgements. Example: "Two students measured the rate of photosynthesis using different methods. Student A counted oxygen bubbles; Student B measured the volume of oxygen collected. Evaluate which method provides more reliable data and explain your reasoning."
