## What Changes and Why

The current MYP framework uses a two-tier concept system. Sixteen key concepts are shared across all subject groups, and each subject also has a list of related concepts specific to the discipline. When planning a unit, teachers select one key concept and one or more related concepts, then weave them into a Statement of Inquiry.

The Enhanced MYP collapses this into a single tier: **specified concepts**. Each subject group receives a curated list of approximately 25 or more concepts. Schools choose from this list and can add their own. There is no prescribed minimum number of concepts to cover across the programme, though eAssessment schools will be assessed on subject-specific specified concepts.

The rationale is straightforward. The key/related distinction created confusion: teachers debated whether "patterns" was being used as a key concept (interdisciplinary) or a related concept (discipline-specific), and the answer rarely affected classroom practice. The new system acknowledges that concepts operate at multiple levels simultaneously, and forces a single, more meaningful choice.

## What the Mathematics Specified Concepts List Likely Includes

The IB has not publicly released the final lists (those come with the September 2027 publication), but the beta trial guidance and IB documentation indicate the mathematics list will include concepts such as:

**From the current key concepts:** Change, Relationships, Logic, Form, Patterns, Systems

**From the current related concepts:** Equivalence, Generalisation, Justification, Measurement, Models, Quantity, Representation, Simplification, Space, Validity

**Likely additions reflecting current mathematical thinking:** Approximation, Proof, Proportionality, Transformation, Visualisation, Estimation, Structure, Variation

The important shift: you no longer need to identify one concept as "key" and another as "related." You simply select the specified concepts that genuinely drive your unit's inquiry.

## What the Sciences Specified Concepts List Likely Includes

Similarly, the sciences list is expected to include:

**From the current key concepts:** Change, Relationships, Systems, Form, Patterns

**From the current related concepts:** Balance, Consequences, Energy, Environment, Evidence, Function, Interaction, Models, Movement, Transformation

**Likely additions:** Cause and Effect, Conservation, Equilibrium, Scale, Adaptation, Sustainability, Innovation, Risk

## How to Transition Your Existing Units

Here is a practical three-step process for converting your current units:

### Step 1: Identify What Drove the Unit

Look at your existing unit planner. Ignore the key/related labels for a moment. Ask: which concepts did students actually grapple with during this unit? Often, the related concept was more central to the learning than the key concept.

**Example — Grade 7 Algebra Unit:**
- Current: Key concept = Relationships, Related concept = Equivalence, Generalisation
- Reality: The unit was really about *generalisation* — students investigated patterns and derived algebraic rules.
- Enhanced MYP: Specified concepts = Generalisation, Patterns. No need to also tag "Relationships" just because it was the prescribed key concept.

### Step 2: Select 1-3 Specified Concepts That Genuinely Fit

Resist the temptation to tag every possible concept. A unit driven by two well-chosen concepts is more coherent than one tagged with five. The concepts should appear in your inquiry question, your assessment tasks, and your teaching — not just in the planner header.

**Test:** If you removed the concept from your unit, would the inquiry fundamentally change? If yes, it belongs. If the unit would proceed identically without it, the concept is decorative, not functional.

### Step 3: Rewrite Your Inquiry Focus

Convert your Statement of Inquiry into an inquiry question or statement. The concepts no longer need to appear explicitly in the wording — they should be embedded in the question's substance.

**Before:** "Generalisation of patterns through logical reasoning allows us to make predictions about unknown cases." (Concepts forced into a sentence.)

**After:** "You have tested five cases and found a rule. Is that enough to be certain — or could the pattern break?" (Concepts — generalisation, patterns, logic — embedded in the question itself.)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to rewrite all my units?**
Not immediately. The conceptual content of your units does not change — only the labelling system. You can transition units gradually between now and September 2027.

**Will my existing resources still work?**
Yes. A Criterion B investigation about pattern generalisation works identically whether the concept is labelled "related concept: generalisation" or "specified concept: generalisation." The student experience is unchanged.

**What if a concept I currently use is not on the specified list?**
Schools can add their own concepts to the list. If your unit genuinely needs a concept that is not specified, add it and document why.

**How does this affect eAssessment?**
eAssessment tasks will be aligned to the specified concepts for each subject. The first new-format eAssessment is May 2030, giving schools three full years to transition.
