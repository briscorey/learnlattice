Teaching philosophy

What I believe about learning

01

Confusion is the entry point, not a sign of failure.

The moment a student says "that doesn't make sense" is the most valuable moment in the lesson. A well-designed problem should leave a room thinking for twenty minutes before anyone needs the teacher.

02

Every student has a mathematical identity that teaching either protects or damages.

By middle school, students have sorted themselves into "math people" and "not math people." That sorting is wrong. Open tasks and low-floor high-ceiling problems are the repair.

03

Precision in planning produces freedom in the classroom.

The most responsive, flexible lessons come from the most rigorous design. When you know exactly what understanding looks like, you can improvise without losing the destination.

Origin story

Why LearnLattice exists

I was planning a Grade 7 proportional reasoning unit and needed tasks that were genuinely open — low enough for students building fraction fluency, high enough for students ready for rate of change. Everything I found was too procedural or too thin on MYP alignment.

So I built the task I needed. Then the next one. After two years of accumulating things I had actually used in a classroom, I built the site to share them. Every resource here has been taught, revised after watching students struggle, and taught again.

The pedagogical design is mine. The classroom testing is mine. AI helped scale the infrastructure — the Hugo framework, interactive games, and the teaching layer tools. One teacher can now build what previously required a team.
