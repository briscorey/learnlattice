{{/* ── THE MOMENT + NUMBERS. story that hooks, stats that ground ── */}}

I drew a staircase on the board. One step: 1 block. Two steps: 3 blocks. Three steps: 6.

I wrote: "How many blocks for 100 steps?"

Then I stopped talking.

For two full minutes, every student was thinking. Not because I told them to. because the question wouldn't let them stop.

That's what I want every lesson to feel like. That's what I build for.

{{/* ── THREE BELIEFS. compact horizontal strip ── */}}
01

Confusion is the entry point.

The moment a student says "that doesn't make sense" is the most valuable moment in the lesson.

02

Every student has a mathematical identity.

Teaching either protects it or damages it. Open tasks and low-floor high-ceiling problems are the repair.

03

Precision in planning produces freedom.

When you know exactly what understanding looks like, you can improvise without losing the destination.

{{/* ── ORIGIN. why this exists ── */}}
Origin

Why LearnLattice exists

I needed tasks that were genuinely open. low enough for students building fraction fluency, high enough for students ready for rate of change. Everything I found was too procedural or too thin on MYP alignment.

So I built the task I needed. Then the next one. After two years of accumulating things I'd actually used in a classroom, I built the site to share them.

The pedagogical design is mine. The classroom testing is mine. AI helped scale the infrastructure. Everything is free and always will be.

Influences

Six thinkers who changed how I teach

Not because I read their books. because I tried their ideas on a Tuesday morning and watched what happened.

Peter Liljedahl Thinking Classrooms

Vertical surfaces, random groups, and the courage to stop talking.

Jo Boaler Mathematical Mindsets

No speed pressure. No "gifted" labels. Low floor, no ceiling.

Dylan Wiliam Formative Assessment

Hinge questions that tell you in real time what a room understands.

Dan Meyer Be Less Helpful

The image before the question. The question before the method.

Seymour Papert Constructionism

Students learn by building. Name it after they've made it.

Jerome Bruner CPA Progression

Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract. Mirrors how understanding forms.
